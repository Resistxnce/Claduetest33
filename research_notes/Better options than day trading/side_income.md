# Side income for a US employee with programming / game-dev (Lua/Luau) skills, outside Roblox, plus 2026 tax basics (research as of 2026-11-11)

Scope note: every figure is dated. "Gross" platform revenue estimates are before platform fees, refunds and taxes. Most product-route datasets only count projects that actually launched (survivorship bias); projects that were never finished are invisible in all of them.

## 1. What do reputable surveys say about typical side-hustle earnings and hours?

### Takeaway
Typical side income is small and part-time: the best probability-weighted survey median is about $200/month (Bankrate, June 2025), while averages ($885 to $1,242/month) are pulled up by a few high earners. The Fed's latest gig data (Oct 2024) shows 20% of adults did some gig activity in the prior month, mostly selling things, and 70% of them spent under 5 hours a week on it. The Fed's 2025 survey (published May 2026) dropped gig questions, so no newer Fed gig figures exist.

### Cited Findings
**Federal Reserve SHED**
- SHED 2024 (survey of 2024, fielded October 2024; report published May 2025): "Overall, 20 percent of adults performed gig activities over the prior month." By type: selling items 13% (previously owned items 10%, items bought to resell 4%, items made or repurposed 3%); short-term tasks 9%; app- or website-arranged platform tasks 4%; short-term rentals 2%. — [Fed SHED 2024, Employment and Gig Work](https://www.federalreserve.gov/publications/2025-economic-well-being-of-us-households-in-2024-employment-and-gig-work.htm)
- SHED 2024: only 21% of gig workers said gig activities were their main job; 51% had a non-gig main job; 70% usually spent under 5 hours/week on gigs; 96% under 35 hours/week. — [Fed SHED 2024](https://www.federalreserve.gov/publications/2025-economic-well-being-of-us-households-in-2024-employment-and-gig-work.htm)
- SHED 2024: 31% of gig workers said they would have trouble making ends meet without gig income; 49% wished pay was more consistent (61% for platform tasks); 55% cited flexible hours. Participation by age: 18–29: 26%; 30–44: 24%; 45–59: 21%; 60+: 12%. The 2024 survey revised the short-term-task questions to match the Census Contingent Worker Supplement, so trend comparisons are imperfect. — [Fed SHED 2024](https://www.federalreserve.gov/publications/2025-economic-well-being-of-us-households-in-2024-employment-and-gig-work.htm)
- SHED 2025 (survey of 2025, fielded Oct 17–28, 2025; report released May 13, 2026). — [Fed press release, May 13, 2026](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/other20260513a.htm)
- The SHED 2025 main report has no gig-work section: a full-text search of the PDF found zero occurrences of "gig". It does report that 58% of self-employed adults said their income varied month to month and 22% struggled to pay bills because of it, versus 28% and 10% of people who work for someone else. The gap persists after controlling for income. — [Fed SHED 2025 report PDF](https://www.federalreserve.gov/publications/files/2025-report-economic-well-being-us-households-202605.pdf)
- SHED 2025 (savings context): 61% of all adults have a tax-preferred retirement account such as a 401(k) or IRA; 37% hold stocks, bonds, ETFs or mutual funds outside a retirement account; 35% of non-retirees think their retirement saving is on track (unchanged from 2024). — [Fed SHED 2025 report PDF](https://www.federalreserve.gov/publications/files/2025-report-economic-well-being-us-households-202605.pdf)

**Private surveys (non-probability online panels; treat as indicative)**
- Bankrate (published July 9, 2025; YouGov, June 2–4, 2025; 2,616 US adults, 699 with a side hustle): 27% of US adults have a side hustle. Side-hustle income averages $885/month, but the **median is $200/month**. Uses: 35% use some for regular living expenses, 41% for discretionary spending, 28% save some, 20% pay down debt. No hours data in the release. — [Bankrate press release](https://www.bankrate.com/press-releases/fewer-americans-have-a-side-hustle-in-2025/)
- Bankrate's prior survey (2024) reportedly had a $250/month median, so the median fell in 2025. This comes from a search summary and was not verified on the 2024 release. — [Bankrate side hustle survey](https://www.bankrate.com/press-releases/fewer-americans-have-a-side-hustle-in-2025/)
- LendingTree 2026 (QuestionPro online survey, March 3–6, 2026; 2,049 consumers aged 18–80; page updated Apr 20, 2026): 33% have a side hustle, down from 38% in 2025 and 44% in 2022. Side hustlers report an **average** of $1,242/month ($1,215 in 2025; $473 in 2022). **No median is published.** Hours: 32% spend 5–10 hrs/week; 24% spend 11–15 hrs/week. 61% say life would be unaffordable without the income. Most common types: gig/on-demand 29%, freelance/professional services 26%, creative/content/media 23%, home improvement/skilled trades 21%. — [LendingTree](https://www.lendingtree.com/debt-consolidation/side-hustle-income-survey/)
- Conflict: a secondary roundup attributes a $400/month median to LendingTree 2026, but LendingTree's own page reports no median. — [voxbooster roundup](https://voxbooster.com/blog/side-hustle-statistics-2026) vs. [LendingTree](https://www.lendingtree.com/debt-consolidation/side-hustle-income-survey/)
- The Penny Hoarder (Feb 2026; 1,000 US adults with *active* side hustles): median $1,275/month (~$15,000/yr); average 13 extra hours/week. These figures come from a search summary and were not verified on the page. The sample excludes people who quit, so it is biased upward. — [The Penny Hoarder](https://www.thepennyhoarder.com/make-money/side-gigs/side-hustle-statistics/)

**JPMorgan Chase Institute (older data; label as such)**
- JPMCI's early online-platform-economy work (mid-2010s data): about 3.1% of adults earned platform income (2.4% capital platforms such as selling or renting, 0.8% labor platforms). Labor-platform earnings were roughly 14–26% of participants' total income depending on the sample definition, and the income was usually supplemental. — [JPMCI, OPE earnings](https://www.jpmorganchase.com/institute/all-topics/labor-markets-and-workforce/insight-online-platform-econ-earnings)
- JPMCI pandemic-era update: nearly 8% of families earned platform income in the year before the pandemic. Platform income as a share of total income was declining while median platform earnings stayed constant. (Pre-2021 data.) — [JPMCI, OPE through the pandemic](https://www.jpmorganchase.com/institute/all-topics/labor-markets-and-workforce/online-platform-economy-through-the-pandemic)

### Inferences
- The median-vs-average gap ($200 vs $885 at Bankrate) is the core signal: the distribution is heavily right-skewed. A planning baseline for a "typical" side hustle is a few hundred dollars a month for under 10 hours a week, not $1,000+.
- Surveys that sample only *current* side hustlers (Penny Hoarder, LendingTree's earnings question) overstate typical outcomes because people who earned little and quit drop out of the sample.
- Fed data suggests the most common "gig" is decluttering (selling previously owned items), which is not a repeatable income stream. Repeatable skilled work is a much smaller slice.
- Income variability is a real cost: self-employed people report much more month-to-month volatility (SHED 2025). That argues for treating side income as savings or investment money rather than budgeting around it.

### Gaps
- No 2025 or 2026 Fed gig-work estimate exists, because SHED 2025 omitted the topic. The Oct 2024 survey is the latest federal read. BLS's Contingent Worker Supplement was not checked in this pass.
- No survey found reports median *hourly* side-hustle earnings net of costs, or medians by side-hustle type (e.g., freelance coding).
- JPMorgan Chase Institute has no post-2021 platform-economy update that I could find.

## 2. Freelance software and game-development work: rates, platform fees, and AI's effect (2025–2026)

### Takeaway
Advertised US Lua developer pay averages about $53/hour (ZipRecruiter, June 2026). But global freelance marketplaces anchor general web and dev work much lower ($15–$50+/hour on Upwork), and Roblox-style scripting commissions are often $20–$25/hour or less. Upwork takes a variable 0–15% fee (since May 2025) and Fiverr a flat 20%. The best evidence says generative AI cut demand for routine coding gigs (about 20% fewer coding postings in the first 8 months after ChatGPT) while raising pay for AI-related and complex work.

### Cited Findings
**Rates**
- ZipRecruiter (as of June 7, 2026): average US hourly pay for "hourly Lua developer" roles is $52.84; most fall between $40.38 and $64.66. These are advertised job-listing rates, not realized freelancer earnings. — [ZipRecruiter](https://www.ziprecruiter.com/Jobs/Hourly-Lua-Developer)
- Flexiple (2025): Roblox developer rates span $20–$150/hour, with most projects near $52–54/hour. This is an aggregator estimate. — [Flexiple](https://flexiple.com/cost-to-hire/roblox-developer)
- Roblox DevForum (anecdotal, self-reported): individual scripters quote around $20–$25/hour; one veteran cites $10–$100 per small commission. — [Roblox DevForum: commission price guide](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/commission-price-guide-for-new-developers/1356888); [DevForum: fair price for scripters](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/what-is-a-fair-price-to-pay-game-builders-and-scripters-in-robux-or-usd/3027798)
- Upwork's own 2026 resources put freelance web developers at $15 to $50+ per hour, with a "Beginner $10–$25/hr" band. Upwork calls these directional benchmarks. Its rate guide could not be fetched (HTTP 403); figures are from search snippets. — [Upwork hourly rates](https://www.upwork.com/resources/upwork-hourly-rates)
- New niche (2026): AI-training and code-evaluation work for Lua/Roblox developers exists. Lifted, an Upwork company, has posted "Senior Lua Developer (Roblox) – AI code evaluation" roles, and a search summary indicates about $60/hour for one senior Lua posting (pay not verified on the listing). — [startup.jobs listing](https://startup.jobs/copy-of-senior-lua-developer-roblox-ai-code-evaluation-lifted-an-upwork-company-8803203)

**Platform fees**
- Upwork replaced its tiered 20%/10%/5% freelancer fee with a **variable 0%–15% fee per contract** in May 2025. The fee is shown at proposal or offer and locked for the contract. Proposals cost Connects at $0.15 each. Source is a third-party breakdown; one other source claims a flat 10%. — [goLance: Upwork fees 2026](https://golance.com/blogs/upwork-fees-explained-2026); [jobbers.io fee comparison](https://www.jobbers.io/freelance-platform-fees-comparison-calculator-2026-the-complete-guide-to-maximizing-your-earnings/)
- Fiverr charges sellers a flat **20%** on all earnings, including tips and extras. Buyers pay an additional fee of about 5.5%. Third-party sources; not verified on Fiverr's help center. — [GigRadar: Upwork vs Fiverr](https://gigradar.io/blog/upwork-vs-fiverr)

**AI's effect on freelance demand and rates**
- Demirci, Hannane & Zhu, "Who Is AI Replacing? The Impact of Generative AI on Online Freelancing Platforms," *Management Science*. Data: ~2 million postings, 61 countries, July 2021–July 2023. Within 8 months of ChatGPT's launch, postings for automation-prone writing and coding jobs fell **21%** relative to manual-intensive jobs (writing −30%, **coding −20%**, image generation −17%). Postings requiring ChatGPT skills grew. — [Management Science (INFORMS)](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/fpi/10.1287/mnsc.2024.05420); [Imperial College summary](https://imperial.ac.uk/business-school/ib-knowledge/technology/how-ai-affecting-freelance-jobs)
- Upwork research (reported June 30, 2025): generative AI is replacing low-complexity, repetitive work while boosting AI-related jobs. Freelance earnings from AI-related work rose **25% year over year**, and gains went mostly to people already experienced in the field. Coverage says demand for basic, repetitive coding declined while experienced developers stayed in demand. This comes from a search summary; the Axios page returned 403. — [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2025/06/30/ai-job-vibe-coding-upwork); [ProPakistani](https://propakistani.pk/2025/07/01/ai-is-helping-freelancers-earn-40-more-than-others/)
- Upwork's causal analysis found generative AI increased total job posts and earnings per new contract overall, but demand fell in writing and translation, especially low-value work. This is platform-produced research. — [Upwork blog](https://www.upwork.com/blog/generative-ai-impact-on-work)
- Upwork (Jan 2025) listed Coding & Web Development among its three fastest-growing skill categories. — [Upwork investor release](https://investors.upwork.com/news-releases/news-release-details/upwork-unveils-2025s-most-demand-skills)
- Hui, Reshef & Zhou ("The Short-Term Effects of Generative AI on Employment") found job postings and earnings for text- and image-based freelancers declined after generative AI launched. — [Economics Online summary](https://www.economicsonline.co.uk/innovation-technology/ai-video-generation-is-lowering-barriers-to-entry-in-the-gig-economy.html/)
- A StaffingIndustry summary of newer Upwork data reports that per-contract earnings for generative and creative work fell 13% even as demand nearly doubled. The context was unclear in the snippet. — [Staffing Industry Analysts](https://www.staffingindustry.com/news/global-daily-news/skilled-ai-freelancing-becomes-more-abundant-lucrative)

### Inferences
- For a US-based Lua/Luau developer, $40–$65/hour (ZipRecruiter) is a realistic ceiling for hourly contract roles. Open marketplaces with global competition and fees of 0–20% often clear lower, especially for generic "script this" tasks that AI tools now do cheaply. After a 10–20% platform fee and roughly 25–35% combined tax (see Q5), a $40/hour gig nets about $22–$27/hour.
- The AI evidence points to a barbell. Commodity tasks (small scripts, bug fixes, simple websites) face falling demand and price pressure. Complex systems work, AI-integration work and AI code-evaluation/training work pay more. Positioning as "experienced game-systems / Luau specialist" or "AI evaluation" is likelier to command US-level rates than competing on small Fiverr gigs.
- Money arrives fastest in services: pay is per milestone or hour with no product risk. The main costs are time spent winning first clients (proposal costs, reviews) and the fee.

### Gaps
- No platform-published median Lua or game-scripting rate (Upwork's guide was inaccessible). No data on median *annual* freelancer earnings on Upwork or Fiverr for US devs.
- No rigorous 2026 study on how AI coding agents changed freelance *rates* (as opposed to postings). The best causal evidence covers 2021–2023, and the 2025 data is platform-produced.
- Payout timing (e.g., Upwork security holds) and Fiverr fee details were not verified on official help pages.

## 3. Building products: Steam/itch.io games, Fortnite UEFN, mobile apps, small SaaS, asset stores

### Takeaway
Product routes have very low medians and long, fat right tails. The median 2025 Steam release grossed about $249–$318 by year-end, roughly 40% did not recoup the $100 listing fee, and about 10% grossed $50K+. About 90% of Fortnite creators earned under $1K in 2024, and about 0.6% earned $100K+. Only 17% of new subscription apps reached $1K/month recurring revenue in 2025. Indie SaaS products that do earn have a median of about $145/month. These are lottery-like distributions that require months of unpaid work before the first dollar.

### Cited Findings
**Steam (Gamalytic estimates, gross)**
- Gamalytic data for games first released Jan 1–Oct 21, 2025 (article Oct 22, 2025): median revenue **$249**, second-lowest since 2020 (2024: $222). **66% earned under $1,000; 9.9% earned $50,000 or more; ~40% did not recoup the $100 Steam fee.** Units sold: 47.5% under 100 copies; 28% 100–1,000; 16% 1,000–10,000; 5.4% 10,000–50,000; 1.2% 50,000–100,000; 1.8% over 100,000. — [80.lv, Oct 22, 2025](https://80.lv/articles/analysts-report-drop-in-game-revenue-on-steam-despite-growing-number-of-releases)
- Full-year view (Dec 21, 2025): 19,606 games released on Steam in 2025 (SteamDB). Gamalytic median revenue per 2025 game is **$318**, and **only 23.4% made over $5,000**. Only 3,186 games (~16%) got over 100 reviews, while 9,550 (~49%) got under 10. Estimated total Steam revenue was $16.2B (vs $15B in 2024). The author notes the data mixes professional, hobbyist, asset-flip and AI-generated games, and that revenue figures are estimates. Top 2025 hits included R.E.P.O. (~$136M) and Schedule I (~$130M). — [Game Dev Report, Dec 21, 2025](https://gamedevreport.beehiiv.com/p/19-606-steam-games-made-16b-in-2025-most-made-0-but-that-s-not-a-problem)
- About 8% of Steam games gross over $100K. The time frame (single year vs lifetime) is unclear across write-ups. — [OpenCritic](https://opencritic.com/news/22399/only-8-of-steam-games-gross-over-100-thousand-40-make-less-than-steam-listing-fees)

**Fortnite UEFN / Creative**
- Epic paid **$352M** to creators in 2024 (up 11% on a March–December comparison), and the creator count rose from ~24,000 (2023) to ~70,000 (2024). — [Epic, Fortnite ecosystem 2024 year in review (Jan 22, 2025)](https://www.fortnite.com/news/fortnite-ecosystem-2024-year-in-review-celebrating-creators-and-looking-ahead); [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2025/01/23/fortnite-epic-games-creator-payouts-2024/)
- Naavik's "State of UGC Games 2026" (Mar 1, 2026) gives the 2024 earnings distribution among ~70,000 creators, citing Epic data:
  - $1M+: 58 creators (0.08%)
  - $100K–$1M: 360 (0.5%)
  - $10K–$100K: ~1,310 (1.9%)
  - $1K–$10K: ~3,000–5,000 (estimate)
  - **Under $1K: ~62,000 (~90%)**
  - The average is about $5,000 per creator per year.
  — [Naavik](https://naavik.co/deep-dives/the-state-of-ugc-games-2026/)
- Naavik (Mar 2026): there is no official 2025 payout total; Naavik models $350–390M (midpoint $370M). Active creators plateaued at ~69,000 in early 2026 while published maps rose from ~198,000 (end 2024) to ~488,000, meaning more competition per creator. Cumulative payouts were $722M as of Sept 2025. — [Naavik](https://naavik.co/deep-dives/the-state-of-ugc-games-2026/)
- Later reports say cumulative UEFN creator payouts passed $1B (date not verified). — [Outlook Respawn](https://respawn.outlookindia.com/gaming/gaming-news/epics-uefn-payouts-reached-1-billion-since-launch)
- 2026 changes (per Naavik):
  - In-island transactions fully launched Jan 9, 2026. Creators get 100% of V-Bucks value (~74% of retail after platform fees) from Dec 2025 through Jan 31, 2027, then 50% (~37% of retail) from Feb 2027.
  - "Sponsored Row" launched Nov 24, 2025; 100% of its revenue goes to the engagement pool through end-2026, then 50%.
  — [Naavik](https://naavik.co/deep-dives/the-state-of-ugc-games-2026/)

**Mobile / subscription apps**
- RevenueCat "State of Subscription Apps 2026" (2025 data; 115,000+ apps, $16B+ revenue): **17% of new apps crossed $1,000/month recurring revenue in 2025**, down from 19% in 2024. — [9to5Mac coverage](https://9to5mac.com/?p=1042212); [RevenueCat SOSA 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2026/)
- RevenueCat 2026: median year-over-year MRR growth was 5.3% while the top 10% of apps grew 306%, so growth is concentrating at the top. — [Subscription Insider](https://www.subscriptioninsider.com/article-type/news/revenuecat-data-shows-subscription-app-growth-concentrating-at-the-top)

**Small software tools / SaaS**
- BigIdeasDB "State of Indie SaaS Revenue 2026" (TrustMRR dataset of 3,787 *revenue-generating* indie SaaS): median **$145/month**, mean $4,298/month. 2025 launch cohort: median $168, with 23.5% over $1K MRR. This is a lower-authority tracking database, not a survey. — [BigIdeasDB](https://bigideasdb.com/state-of-indie-saas-revenue-2026)

**Asset stores**
- Epic's Fab marketplace: creators receive **88%** of revenue from product sales (88/12 split). A 100% promo ran from launch (Oct 2024) to end-2024. — [Fab documentation (Epic)](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/fab); [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/marketing/epic-to-unify-content-marketplaces-and-offer-creators-88-percent-revenue-cut)
- Unity Asset Store is listed at a 70% publisher share by a third-party comparison site, not verified with Unity. The same site and a seller blog claim Fab moved to a tiered 70–80% payout in Sept 2026; this is uncorroborated and absent from Epic's docs. — [tech-insider comparison](https://tech-insider.org/unity-asset-store-vs-fab-vs-cgtrader-2026/); [StraySpark seller retrospective](https://www.strayspark.studio/blog/fab-marketplace-12-month-retrospective-seller-2026)

### Inferences
- **Odds of a meaningful outcome, by route (all among launched products only):**

  | Route (year) | Typical outcome | Share reaching meaningful money |
  |---|---|---|
  | Steam game (2025 releases) | Median $249–$318 gross by year-end | ~10% grossed $50K+; ~23% grossed over $5K |
  | Fortnite creator (2024) | ~90% earned under $1K | ~2.5% earned $10K+; ~0.6% earned $100K+; 0.08% earned $1M+ |
  | Subscription app (2025 new apps) | — | 17% reached $1K/month recurring |
  | Indie SaaS (with revenue) | Median ~$145/month | ~23.5% of the 2025 cohort exceeded $1K/month |

  Because none of these datasets count unfinished projects, the true odds per *attempt* are lower.
- Steam medians are "revenue to date" for the release year, so late-2025 releases had little time to sell. This partly explains the $249 (Oct cutoff) vs $318 (Dec cutoff) gap. Gross figures also overstate developer take-home: the platform cut, refunds and taxes are not deducted.
- Time to first dollar is long for products (months of unpaid development, then launch). Startup cash costs are modest: the Steam fee is $100, Fab and UEFN have no listed upfront fee in these sources, and time is the real cost. A product route suits someone who treats it as a learning or portfolio project with lottery-ticket upside, not as reliable monthly income.
- Fortnite's 2026 in-island transaction share (100% of V-Bucks value until Jan 2027, then 50%) is a time-limited window. Engagement payouts remain extremely concentrated, and map count per creator has risen sharply.

### Gaps
- itch.io: no revenue-distribution data found.
- No VG Insights 2025/2026 distribution was retrieved; Gamalytic is the sole tracker cited, and its revenue is modeled.
- No official Epic 2025 payout total or 2025 earnings distribution.
- No median first-year revenue for mobile apps (RevenueCat's full 330+ page report was not parsed). No ad-supported (non-subscription) mobile game distribution.
- No earnings distribution for asset-store sellers (Unity or Fab).
- MicroConf's State of Independent SaaS survey figures could not be accessed.

## 4. Lower-skill options with proven earnings: tutoring, content creation, reselling, gig apps

### Takeaway
These routes pay modestly but predictably. Tutors' median pay was $19.27/hour (BLS, 2024). Rideshare nets roughly $12–$23/hour after vehicle costs depending on who funded the study (independent estimates are at the low end). YouTube requires 1,000 subscribers plus 4,000 watch hours (8,000 for new applicants from Feb 1, 2027) before any ad revenue. Reselling is common, but much of it is one-off decluttering.

### Cited Findings
**Tutoring / teaching**
- BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook: tutors' 2024 median pay was $40,090/year, or **$19.27/hour**, across 215,500 jobs. This covers employed tutors in all subjects, not independent coding tutors. — [BLS OOH: Tutors](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/education-training-and-library/tutors.htm)

**Content creation (YouTube)**
- Current YouTube Partner Program (YPP) ad-revenue thresholds: 1,000 subscribers plus either 4,000 hours of public watch time in 12 months or 10 million Shorts views over 90 days. — [Tubefilter, Aug 10, 2026](https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/08/10/youtube-partner-program-ad-eligibility-requirements-shorts/); [YouTube Help: YPP overview](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/72851?rd=1)
- From **Feb 1, 2027**:
  - New applicants need 8,000 watch hours over 365 days or 20 million Shorts views over 90 days; existing members are grandfathered.
  - Shorts ads will be limited to creators with at least 10 million Shorts views over 90 days.
  - Fan-funding features remain at 500 subscribers plus 3,000 watch hours or 3M Shorts views.
  — [Tubefilter, Aug 10, 2026](https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/08/10/youtube-partner-program-ad-eligibility-requirements-shorts/)
- Revenue split (third-party sources; Google's page not fully verified): creators get 55% of ad revenue on long-form video and 45% of allocated Shorts revenue. — [Shopify blog](https://www.shopify.com/blog/youtube-shorts-monetization); [AIR Media-Tech](https://air.io/en/monetization/youtube-partner-program-requirements-2026-the-complete-guide)

**Reselling**
- In the Fed's October 2024 survey, 13% of adults sold items in the prior month: 10% previously owned items, 4% items bought specifically to resell, 3% items made or repurposed. — [Fed SHED 2024](https://www.federalreserve.gov/publications/2025-economic-well-being-of-us-households-in-2024-employment-and-gig-work.htm)

**Gig apps (rideshare), net hourly pay**
- EPI (2018; older): average Uber driver compensation after commissions and vehicle expenses was **$11.77/hour** from a gross of $24.77/hour. That falls to $10.87 after self-employment payroll taxes and $9.21 as a W-2 equivalent with basic benefits. — [EPI](https://www.epi.org/?p=145552)
- Illinois EPI / University of Illinois (2019 data; older): Chicago drivers averaged $12.30/hour after vehicle expenses and payroll taxes. — [ILEPI/PMCR report](https://news.wttw.com/sites/default/files/article/file-attachments/ilepi-pmcr-on-demand-workers-sub-minimum-wages-final.pdf)
- Gridwise (driver-app data, 2025, published 2026): median Uber driver $21.18/hour in trip pay ($21.92 with bonuses), **before** gas, insurance and vehicle costs. Gridwise estimates net at roughly $15–$18/hour without a published method. — [Gridwise](https://gridwise.io/blog/uber-driver-pay/how-much-do-uber-drivers-make)
- HR&A Advisors (Uber-commissioned, Nov 2025): net earnings of $21.29–$23.01/hour across Chicago, Philadelphia and Portland. Philadelphia: $27.83 gross minus $6.54/hour estimated expenses. This is industry-funded. — [HR&A Philadelphia](https://www.hraadvisors.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/HRA_Uber-Rideshare-Driver-Net-Earnings-Study_2025_Philadelphia.pdf); [HR&A Chicago](https://www.hraadvisors.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/HRA_Uber-Rideshare-Driver-Net-Earnings-Study_2025_Chicago.pdf)
- An academic study on eScholarship (University of California) reports far lower net pay: a median of $5.93/hour in California after expenses and excluding tips, in 2022 dollars. The snippet was not fully verified. — [eScholarship](https://escholarship.org/content/qt91m253q8/qt91m253q8.pdf)

### Inferences
- Net rideshare pay is highly sensitive to whether idle time and full vehicle depreciation are counted. Independent estimates ($9–$12/hour, 2018–2019) sit well below industry-funded ones ($21–$23/hour, 2025). A cautious planning figure is roughly $12–$18/hour net before income tax, which is lower than skilled freelance work.
- For someone who can code, tutoring or teaching coding likely beats the $19.27 BLS median, but no source here quantifies coding-tutor rates. Gig apps and tutoring are the fastest to first dollar (days to weeks) with near-zero startup cost, except the car for rideshare.
- YouTube has a hard pre-revenue barrier (4,000 watch hours now, 8,000 for new applicants from Feb 2027). Content is a long-horizon, high-variance bet, like the product routes.

### Gaps

- No data on reseller profit margins or hourly earnings.
- No data on independent or online coding-tutor rates, or on platform commissions (Wyzant, Varsity Tutors, etc.).
- No 2025–2026 *independent* (non-industry-funded) national estimate of rideshare or delivery net hourly pay.

## 5. Taxes on side income for 2026

### Takeaway
Side profit is subject to 15.3% self-employment tax (12.4% Social Security up to the $184,500 wage base for 2026, shared with W-2 wages, plus 2.9% Medicare) on top of ordinary income tax. For a 22%-bracket earner, roughly 30% of each marginal dollar of profit goes to federal tax even with the 20% QBI deduction. The 2025 tax law restored the 1099-K threshold to over $20,000 **and** over 200 transactions (retroactively) and raised the 1099-NEC threshold to $2,000 for 2026 payments. All income stays taxable whether or not a form is issued. Self-employment income opens Solo 401(k)/SEP saving (up to $72,000 total for 2026), but the $24,500 employee deferral is shared with any day-job 401(k).

### Cited Findings
**Self-employment tax**
- 2026 Social Security wage base: $184,500 (up from $176,100 in 2025). Maximum Social Security portion of SE tax: $22,878 (12.4% × $184,500). Medicare's 2.9% has no cap. — [Journal of Accountancy, Oct 2025](https://www.journalofaccountancy.com/news/2025/oct/social-security-wage-base-and-cola-announced-for-2026/); [Mercer Advisors](https://www.merceradvisors.com/taxes/social-security-wage-base-2026-increase-employer-guide/)

**Form 1099-K (payment apps / marketplaces)**
- IRS page (last updated Oct 28, 2026): payment apps and online marketplaces report when payments "exceed $20,000 in more than 200 transactions." Gifts and repayments from friends and family should not be reported on a 1099-K. "You must still report any income on your tax return" whether or not you get a 1099-K. — [IRS: Understanding your Form 1099-K](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/understanding-your-form-1099-k)
- The One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBBA) "retroactively reinstated the reporting threshold in effect prior to the passage of the American Rescue Plan Act of 2021." This superseded the prior IRS phase-in ($5,000 for 2024, $2,500 for 2025, $600 for 2026+). IRS FAQs are in Fact Sheet 2025-08 (Oct 23, 2025). — [CPA Practice Advisor, Oct 23, 2025](https://www.cpapracticeadvisor.com/2025/10/23/irs-issues-new-faqs-on-20000-form-1099-k-threshold-under-obbba/171575/)
- Both conditions must be met: the IRS wording is payments that "exceed $20,000 in more than 200 transactions." A search summary of secondary tax-prep sources applies this explicitly to calendar years 2025 and 2026. — [IRS: Understanding your Form 1099-K](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/understanding-your-form-1099-k); [TaxAct](https://blog.taxact.com/4-common-misconceptions-about-the-new-1099-k-reporting-threshold/)

**Form 1099-NEC / 1099-MISC**
- For payments made in 2026, the reporting threshold is **$2,000**, replacing the $600 level in place since 1954. It is inflation-indexed for payments after Dec 31, 2026 (starting calendar 2027). Payments under $2,000 also aren't subject to backup withholding. — [IRS Publication 1099 (2026)](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p1099); [IRS Instructions for Forms 1099-MISC/NEC](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/i1099mec.pdf)

**Estimated taxes**
- You generally must make estimated payments if you expect to owe at least $1,000 after withholding and refundable credits. You avoid the underpayment penalty if withholding plus timely estimates cover the smaller of 90% of this year's tax or 100% of last year's tax (110% if prior-year AGI exceeded $150,000, or $75,000 if married filing separately). — [IRS: Estimated taxes](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/estimated-taxes); [IRS Publication 505 (2026)](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p505); [IRS 2026 Form 1040-ES](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/f1040es.pdf)

**QBI deduction**
- OBBBA makes the qualified business income (QBI) deduction permanent, modifies its phase-in limits, and adds an inflation-adjusted minimum deduction. IRS materials call these provisions the "Working Families Tax Cuts." — [IRS: Working Families Tax Cuts, business provisions](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/understanding-the-working-families-tax-cuts-business-tax-provisions-youtube-video-text-script)

**Retirement accounts (2026 limits)**
- IRS (IR-2025-111, Nov 13, 2025): 401(k) employee deferral **$24,500** (from $23,500); age-50+ catch-up $8,000; ages 60–63 catch-up $11,250; IRA limit **$7,500** (from $7,000). Technical guidance is in Notice 2025-67. — [IRS news release](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/401k-limit-increases-to-24500-for-2026-ira-limit-increases-to-7500)
- IRA catch-up (50+): $1,100 for 2026, for a total of $8,600. — [IRS COLA table](https://www.irs.gov/retirement-plans/cola-increases-for-dollar-limitations-on-benefits-and-contributions)
- Total annual additions limit (Solo 401(k) / SEP) for 2026: **$72,000**. A SEP contribution is the lesser of 25% of compensation (about 20% of net self-employment earnings for an owner) or $72,000. — [SelfEmployed.com](https://www.selfemployed.com/news/solo-401k-sep-ira-limits-72000-2026/); [VisaVerge](https://www.visaverge.com/news/2026-retirement-plan-limits-401k-deferral-at-24500-sep-at-72000-simple-ira-at-17000/)
- IRS on one-participant 401(k)s:
  - Available to a business owner with no employees (or owner plus spouse).
  - Elective deferrals up to 100% of compensation, subject to the annual limit, plus employer nonelective contributions up to 25% of compensation.
  - Self-employed "earned income" is net SE earnings after deducting half of SE tax and the contributions themselves.
  - Crucially for someone with a day job: "limits on elective deferrals are by person, not by plan."
  - Form 5500-EZ is generally required once plan assets reach $250,000.
  — [IRS: One-participant 401(k) plans](https://www.irs.gov/retirement-plans/one-participant-401k-plans)

### Inferences
- **Worked example (my arithmetic).** Assumptions: single filer, 22% marginal bracket, W-2 wages below the $184,500 wage base, 20% QBI deduction applies unrestricted, standard Schedule SE mechanics (SE tax on 92.35% of net profit; half of SE tax deductible), no state tax.
  - For $500 of extra net profit: SE tax $70.65; income tax $81.78 after the half-SE-tax deduction ($35.32) and QBI deduction ($92.94).
  - Total federal tax is **$152.43 (30.5%)**, so you keep about $348.
  - Without QBI the rate is 34.6%; in the 12% bracket it is 23.1%.
  - If W-2 wages already exceed $184,500, only the 2.9% Medicare portion applies, so total federal tax is about 27.9% even in the 32% bracket.
  - The 92.35% multiplier, half-SE deduction and $400 Schedule SE filing threshold are standard rules from background knowledge, not re-fetched here; verify on IRS Topic 554 and Schedule SE.
- **Practical rule of thumb:** set aside about 25–35% of side *profit* for federal tax, plus state tax. A W-2 employee can often avoid quarterly 1040-ES payments by raising day-job withholding (W-4) to hit the 100%/110% safe harbor. Withholding is treated as paid evenly through the year; that point is background knowledge, so verify in Pub 505.
- **The 1099 thresholds change paperwork, not tax.** With the 1099-K at over $20,000 and over 200 transactions, and the 1099-NEC at $2,000, many small side earners will get no form at all, but the income is still reportable. Keep your own records.
- **Retirement-account interaction for an employee.** If the day-job 401(k) deferral is already maxed ($24,500), a Solo 401(k) can still take employer profit-sharing contributions. That is roughly 20% of (net profit − half SE tax): about $1,115 on $6,000 of profit, $2,230 on $12,000, $4,461 on $24,000 (my arithmetic). A SEP-IRA gives the same ~20% employer amount with simpler admin. With modest side profit, these contributions are small, so the $7,500 IRA (Roth or traditional) may matter more. Retirement contributions defer income tax but do not reduce SE tax.
- **Deductible expenses.** Ordinary and necessary business costs (software and tools, platform fees, a business share of hardware, Steam/Fab fees, home office if used regularly and exclusively) reduce Schedule C profit and therefore both SE and income tax. This comes from background knowledge and was not fetched in this pass; verify with IRS Publication 334 and Publication 587.

### Gaps
- Not verified on IRS pages this pass: the exact 2026 quarterly due dates; the QBI phase-in thresholds and minimum-deduction amount; hobby-vs-business rules; home-office details.
- State 1099-K thresholds: several states historically used lower thresholds, but none were checked.
- The 2027 inflation-adjusted 1099-NEC threshold was not retrieved.
- Roth IRA income limits for 2026 were not retrieved.

## 6. The math of earning more vs. investing more

### Takeaway
For someone with a small portfolio, an extra $500/month of side income dwarfs plausible investment returns: $6,000/year equals the expected annual gain on about $86,000 at 7%. Investing that $500/month compounds to about $87K in 10 years, $260K in 20 and $610K in 30 at 7% nominal (no taxes or fees). After roughly 30% federal tax on side profit, about $348/month invested grows to about $60K, $181K and $424K. Raising income and savings rate matters far more at small balances than squeezing higher returns, which is the core argument against day trading a small account.

### Cited Findings
- Long-run anchor: Damodaran's S&P 500 total-return series (including dividends; updated Jan 5, 2026, through 2025) implies about **10.0%/year compound nominal for 1928–2025** ($100 grew to $1,157,598.95). It also implies ~11.9%/year for 1976–2025 and ~14.7%/year for 2016–2025. These rates are derived from the cumulative-value column; I verified 1928–2025 at 10.02%. Past returns are not guaranteed, and real (inflation-adjusted) returns are lower. — [Damodaran, NYU Stern historical returns](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/New_Home_Page/datafile/histretSP.html)
- Only 37% of US adults hold stocks, bonds, ETFs or mutual funds outside retirement accounts, and 61% have a tax-preferred retirement account (SHED, survey of 2025). — [Fed SHED 2025 report PDF](https://www.federalreserve.gov/publications/files/2025-report-economic-well-being-us-households-202605.pdf)

### Inferences
**Assumptions for my arithmetic:** contributions at the end of each month; monthly compounding of the stated annual nominal return; no taxes, fees or inflation adjustment unless stated.

**Future value of $500/month invested:**

| Annual return | 10 years | 20 years | 30 years |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0% (cash under mattress) | $60,000 | $120,000 | $180,000 |
| 4% | $73,625 | $183,387 | $347,025 |
| 6% | $81,940 | $231,020 | $502,258 |
| 7% | $86,542 | $260,463 | $609,985 |
| 8% | $91,473 | $294,510 | $745,180 |
| 10% (≈ S&P 500 1928–2025 nominal) | $102,422 | $379,684 | $1,130,244 |

Contributions total $60,000, $120,000 and $180,000 at 10, 20 and 30 years; the rest is growth. At 7% over 30 years, growth is $429,985, about 70% of the end value.

**Portfolio needed to produce $6,000/year (= $500/month) from returns:**
- At 4%: $150,000
- At 6%: $100,000
- At 7%: $85,714
- At 8%: $75,000
- At 10%: $60,000

**Expected annual gain on small portfolios:**

| Balance | 4% | 7% | 10% |
|---|---|---|---|
| $5,000 | $200 | $350 | $500 |
| $10,000 | $400 | $700 | $1,000 |
| $25,000 | $1,000 | $1,750 | $2,500 |
| $50,000 | $2,000 | $3,500 | $5,000 |

Even an exceptional 20% year on $10,000 is $2,000, about 4 months of a $500/month side income.

**After-tax version:** at a ~30.5% federal rate on side profit (22%-bracket example in Q5), $500 of profit leaves about $348/month. Invested at 6%, 7% and 8%:

| Annual return | 10 years | 20 years | 30 years |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6% | $56,959 | $160,591 | $349,139 |
| 7% | $60,159 | $181,058 | $424,024 |
| 8% | $63,586 | $204,725 | $518,003 |

Routing side profit into a Solo 401(k), SEP or traditional IRA defers income tax (not SE tax). A Roth IRA ($7,500 limit in 2026) shelters future growth.

**Implications:**
- At small balances, the savings rate dominates: one extra $500/month adds more each year than a 7% return on anything under ~$86K.
- The investment return dominates only after the portfolio grows large. With steady $500/month investing, cumulative growth first exceeds cumulative contributions after about:
  - 12.8 years at 10%
  - 15.8 years at 8%
  - 18.1 years at 7%
  - 21.1 years at 6%
  - 31.6 years at 4%

  (My arithmetic. At 7%: 10 years gives $26.5K growth vs $60K contributed; 20 years gives $140K vs $120K.) For the first one to two decades, how much you contribute matters more than the return.
- The comparison assumes the side income is reliable. Q1 survey medians ($200/month at Bankrate) suggest many people earn far less than $500/month, and product routes (Q3) usually earn less than their hours would earn at a wage. The robust options for steady $500+/month are skilled hourly services or a part-time, W-2-like gig.

### Gaps
- No credible published source was found that directly compares "earn more" vs "invest more" for small accounts. The arithmetic above is mine, with stated assumptions.
- The figures do not net out inflation (real returns are lower; historical US inflation was not retrieved) or fund fees.
- The tax rate in the after-tax example depends on bracket and state.
