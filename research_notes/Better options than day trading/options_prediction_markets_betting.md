# How regular people fare with options trading (incl. 0DTE), prediction markets, and sports betting: evidence as of Nov 11, 2026

Scope: US only. All figures are dated. "Older" means the data was collected before 2024. Where sources conflict, both are shown. Secondary aggregators and affiliate sites are labeled as such.

## Q1. Options trading (especially short-dated and 0DTE): how it works, growth, costs, retail outcomes, regulator warnings

### Takeaway
US listed options volume set its sixth straight annual record in 2025 (about +24%). Same-day (0DTE) S&P 500 options are now the main retail product. Most academic studies using retail proxies find that retail options traders lose money on average, mainly through bid-ask spreads, especially on cheap, short-dated, single-leg contracts they buy. A Cboe-funded 2025 paper disputes the biggest of these studies and finds no statistically significant loss on Cboe customer trades held to expiration. The evidence is therefore "negative on average for buyers of short-dated options, after costs," not "every retail trader loses."

### Cited Findings
**How it works and what FINRA warns about (FINRA page dated June 4, 2026)**
- 0DTE options expire the same day they are traded. FINRA warns that "any strategy that can quickly earn profits can quickly bring losses as well," and that 0DTE options are "very sensitive to changes in the price of the asset underlying the option." — [FINRA, "Zeroing In on an Options Trading Strategy: 0DTE" (June 4, 2026)](https://www.finra.org/investors/insights/zeroing-in-options-trading-strategy)
- FINRA also warns of the following. Buyers "could lose the entire premium paid." Writers of uncovered calls face "unlimited potential loss." A broker may liquidate a position near the close, which can cut profits or cause losses. Intraday margin rules apply, and options trading requires specific approval from the brokerage. It points investors to the Options Disclosure Document ("Characteristics and Risks of Standardized Options"). Its worked examples show a $4,000 net loss on a sold call and a $2,000 premium lost on a bought call that expired worthless. FINRA's page cites no statistics. — [FINRA (June 4, 2026)](https://www.finra.org/investors/insights/zeroing-in-options-trading-strategy)
- History of the products: Cboe began weekly Friday expirations in 2005 and added Monday and Wednesday expirations in 2016. Since May 2022, S&P 500 options expire every weekday. — [Beckmeyer, Branger & Gayda, version Dec 15, 2023 (PDF)](https://wp.lancs.ac.uk/fofi2024/files/2024/04/FoFI-2024-146-Leander-Gayda.pdf)

**Volume growth**
- The OCC reported a 24% year-on-year rise in cleared volume in 2025, the sixth consecutive record year. — [FOW, Jan 6, 2026](https://www.fow.com/insights/us-options-volume-grows-a-quarter-in-2025-to-new-record)
- An aggregator citing a Jan 5, 2026 OCC report gives these figures: 15.21 billion contracts in 2025 (+24.4% vs 2024), about 61 million contracts per day, and a single-day record of 110 million contracts on Oct 10, 2025. A tracker reports average daily volume of about 68.9 million through April 2026. One source gives 16.89 billion contracts for 2025, which conflicts with the OCC-based figure and should be discounted. These are secondary; verify against the [OCC 2025 Annual Report](https://annualreport.theocc.com/). — [VolRadar options statistics (aggregator)](https://volradar.com/options-trading-statistics); [Options Trading Statistics 2026 (aggregator)](https://joincfu.com/blog/options-trading-statistics)
- 0DTE share of S&P 500 (SPX) options volume is reported at about 59% in one source and a record 66.2% in July 2026 (citing Cboe data) in another. Both are secondary, and the periods and definitions differ. — [VolRadar (aggregator)](https://volradar.com/options-trading-statistics); [joincfu (aggregator)](https://joincfu.com/blog/options-trading-statistics)
- NYSE's Q3 2025 industry update: the "customer" segment accounted for almost 46% of options volume, the highest since Q4 2021. Note that "customer" includes institutions, not only retail. — [NYSE/ICE Options Industry Landscape, Q3 2025](https://www.nyse.com/publicdocs/nyse/NYSE_Research_Q3_2025_Options_Industry_Update.pdf)

**Academic evidence on retail options outcomes**
- **Bryzgalova, Pavlova & Sikorskaya, Journal of Finance 78(6), 2023 (pandemic-era data, Nov 2019 to June 2021; older finding).**
  - Retail options trading grew rapidly, helped by payment for order flow. The retail share reached "over 60% of total market volume" per the abstract as returned by search; I could not open the full text to confirm.
  - Most PFOF came from three wholesalers: Citadel, Susquehanna and Wolverine.
  - Retail investors "prefer cheaper, weekly options with average bid-ask spread of 12.6%, and lose money on average." — [LBS Research Online](https://lbsresearch.london.edu/id/eprint/2827); [LBS summary](https://www.london.edu/faculty-and-research/academic-research/r/retail-trading-in-options-and-the-rise-of-the-big-three-wholesalers)
  - Co-author Pavlova's slides summarize the short-term result: retail buyers of short-term options lose money, and writers of short-term options make money. — [Pavlova presentation (LSE Systemic Risk Centre, 2023)](https://www.systemicrisk.ac.uk/sites/default/files/2023-03/Anna%20Pavlova%20LSE.pdf)
  - Their loss estimates, as summarized by a later paper: assuming a 10-day horizon, the aggregate retail portfolio lost an average of −$5.03 million per day (about $2.1 billion in total). Assuming options were held to expiration, the loss was −$1.63 million per day. — [Amaya, Garcia-Ares, Pearson & Vasquez (Apr 7, 2025), Cboe-hosted PDF](https://cdn.cboe.com/resources/education/research_publications/Retail_Profitability.pdf)
- **de Silva, Smith & So, "Losing is Optional: Retail Option Trading and Expected Announcement Volatility" (working paper 2022; published in Review of Finance, 2025; older data).**
  - Retail investors pile into options before earnings announcements, especially those expected to be volatile. They are slow to close after volatility collapses.
  - Estimated average losses are 5% to 9%, and 10% to 14% for high-expected-volatility announcements. Market makers are the beneficiaries.
  - Caveat: this study treats all "customer" volume as retail. — [Stanford GSB](https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/faculty-research/publications/losing-optional-retail-option-trading-expected-announcement); [MIT Sloan summary](https://mitsloan.mit.edu/ideas-made-to-matter/retail-investors-lose-big-options-markets-research-shows)
- **Beckmeyer, Branger & Gayda, "Retail Traders Love 0DTE Options... But Should They?" (SSRN working paper; version Dec 15, 2023; data Feb 2021 to Sep 2023).**
  - More than 75% of identified retail trades in S&P 500 options are 0DTE.
  - Retail investors lost $241,000 on an average day, rising to $350,000 per day after daily expirations began on May 16, 2022. Total losses exceeded $125 million, of which more than $90 million came from transaction costs.
  - Roughly 60% of daily losses came from transaction costs, and about 60% from 0DTE put positions. Retail buys performed particularly poorly.
  - Single-leg positions lost more than $100 million. Multi-leg spreads did better: median margin-adjusted returns were 3% for put spreads and 3.3% for call spreads, although multi-leg trades also lost money in aggregate.
  - 74.2% of retail orders were for a single contract. — [Beckmeyer, Branger & Gayda PDF](https://wp.lancs.ac.uk/fofi2024/files/2024/04/FoFI-2024-146-Leander-Gayda.pdf); [SSRN 4404704](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4404704)
  - The earlier March 2023 version reported $184,000 per day on average and $358,000 per day after May 2022. — [WealthManagement.com](https://www.wealthmanagement.com/equities/retail-investors-are-in-love-with-same-day-options)
  - Limitation: their retail proxy (orders of up to 10 contracts executed in price-improvement auctions) covers only about 2% to 4% of S&P 500 option trading (over 6% for 0DTE). The dollar figures are therefore for a slice of retail activity, not the total. — [same PDF](https://wp.lancs.ac.uk/fofi2024/files/2024/04/FoFI-2024-146-Leander-Gayda.pdf)
- **Counter-evidence: Amaya, Garcia-Ares, Pearson & Vasquez, "New Evidence on the Performance of Customer Options Trades" (Apr 7, 2025; funded by a data research grant from Cboe's Options Institute).**
  - They used Cboe data that records true trade direction and valued positions at expiration, for January 2020 to June 2023.
  - Cboe customer trades in single-leg price-improvement auctions (SLIMs) earned about +$0.34 million per day (t-statistic 0.29). For January 2020 to June 2021 the figure was +$2.07 million per day (t = 1.19). Neither is statistically significant. The authors conclude "no evidence that Cboe customer SLIM trades are unprofitable."
  - Bought calls and sold puts made money, while bought puts and sold calls lost money. The S&P 500 rose 33% to 38% over their sample periods.
  - Losses at the intraday horizon (about $1 million per day under the earlier method) were statistically significant.
  - About 42% of trades closed existing positions.
  - They argue the 0DTE paper's proxy captures only 4% to 6% of SPX trading.
  - They also note two other studies that find customer losses: Naranjo, Nimalendran & Wu (2024) on complex trades, and Bogousslavsky & Muravyev (2025), whose roughly 3,000 traders on a social investing platform had small average losses on single-leg trades. — [Cboe-hosted PDF](https://cdn.cboe.com/resources/education/research_publications/Retail_Profitability.pdf)

**Typical bid-ask spread costs**
- Retail-proxy (SLIM) trades, January to June 2021 (older):
  - Average quoted spread was about 13.9% to 14.1% of the option's mid-price, and the effective spread about 6.1% to 6.6%.
  - For trades under $250 of premium: quoted about 23.7%, effective about 10.8% to 11.4%.
  - For 0DTE: quoted about 10.6% to 11.7%, effective about 6.3% to 7.1%.
  - For out-of-the-money options (moneyness −1 to −0.1): quoted about 28% to 29%, effective about 12.6% to 13.7%.
  - About 46% of trades were for a single contract.
  - The paper defines effective spread as 2 × |trade price − midpoint| / midpoint. — [Amaya et al. 2025, Table 1](https://cdn.cboe.com/resources/education/research_publications/Retail_Profitability.pdf)
- 0DTE retail orders, 2021 to 2023: effective spread was 6.0% for calls and 5.0% for puts, versus 12.5% and 9.6% for non-retail trades. Price improvement roughly halves the cost but does not eliminate it. — [Beckmeyer et al. PDF](https://wp.lancs.ac.uk/fofi2024/files/2024/04/FoFI-2024-146-Leander-Gayda.pdf)
- Average bid-ask spread of 12.6% on the weekly options retail prefers (2019 to 2021 data). — [Bryzgalova et al. (LBS)](https://lbsresearch.london.edu/id/eprint/2827)
- Commission-free brokers still pass through regulatory and clearing fees. Brokers that charge commissions add per-contract fees on top of spreads. — [Beckmeyer et al. PDF](https://wp.lancs.ac.uk/fofi2024/files/2024/04/FoFI-2024-146-Leander-Gayda.pdf)

### Inferences
- Under that definition, the one-way cost of crossing the spread is about half the effective spread. A 6% effective spread costs about 3% of the premium to open and about another 3% to close early, so roughly 6% round trip. For premiums under $250 (about 11% effective), the round-trip cost is roughly 11%. A 0DTE buyer who trades in and out repeatedly faces this cost every time.
- Before costs, options are close to zero-sum between buyer and seller. The consistent findings (spread costs, buyer losses on short-dated and lottery-like contracts, market makers and wholesalers as counterparties) suggest a typical small, short-dated, single-leg buyer has negative expected value after costs. The Cboe-funded paper shows that results depend on market direction and on method. It does not show that retail buyers have an edge.
- The most damaging pattern identified across papers is buying cheap, out-of-the-money, short-dated options, especially around events and with puts in a rising market. That is where spreads as a share of premium are widest.

## Q2. Prediction markets (Kalshi, Polymarket, broker-run): how they work, volume, regulation as of Nov 2026, fees, who profits, favorite-longshot bias

### Takeaway
Prediction markets let users trade yes/no contracts that pay $1 if an event happens. Since 2025 they have become a mass-market sports-betting substitute, with about $50 billion per month in combined Kalshi and Polymarket volume by mid-2026. Before fees they are zero-sum between traders. The best academic evidence (Kalshi, 2021 to April 2025) shows a strong favorite-longshot bias: cheap "longshot" contracts lose more than 60% on average, and takers do far worse than makers. On-chain Polymarket studies show profits concentrated in fewer than 1% of wallets. As of Nov 11, 2026, legality of sports contracts is unresolved. Federal circuits are split (the Third Circuit sided with Kalshi; the Sixth and Ninth sided with the states), the CFTC backs the platforms and is suing states, and New Jersey's Supreme Court petition (No. 26-299) is pending.

### Cited Findings
**How they work**
- Kalshi received a CFTC license as a designated contract market in November 2020. It is a quote-driven market with prices from 1¢ to 99¢. A contract pays $1 if the event happens.
  - "Makers" post offers and "Takers" accept them, and Kalshi's data records which side is which.
  - Markets cover culture, weather, economics, politics, and (since early 2025) sports. There are no stake limits, unlike older markets such as PredictIt and the Iowa Electronic Markets.
  - Example from the paper: a 32¢ contract paid $1 if April 2025 CPI inflation came in above 0.3%. — [Bürgi, Deng & Whelan, "Makers and Takers," Jan 2026 version (PDF)](https://www.karlwhelan.com/Papers/Kalshi.pdf); [SSRN 5502658](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5502658)
- "Prior to fees, Kalshi participants simply swap money, so by definition the average return prior to fees across the total volume of money invested is zero." — [Bürgi, Deng & Whelan (Jan 2026)](https://www.karlwhelan.com/Papers/Kalshi.pdf)
- Polymarket runs an offshore main platform and a separate US platform, Polymarket US. The Block tracks the two separately. Polymarket's route back into the US was its acquisition of QCEX, a CFTC-regulated exchange. A tracker listing seen in search results labels Polymarket US as "QCX LLC · CFTC DCM"; I could not confirm which site published it. Rutgers statistician Harry Crane estimated that US traders drove about 30% of the offshore platform's volume in the 12 months to Apr 30, 2026. — [The Block (Aug 3, 2026)](https://www.theblock.co/news/web3/2026-08-03-kalshi-polymarket-volume-july-410382); [RotoWire timeline (QCEX acquisition)](https://www.rotowire.com/prediction-markets/legal-timeline)

**Fees**
- Kalshi's taker fee is $0.07 × P × (1 − P) per contract, where P is the price in dollars, rounded up to the next cent. Kalshi charged makers nothing until April 2025 and began charging maker fees afterwards. — [Bürgi, Deng & Whelan (Jan 2026)](https://www.karlwhelan.com/Papers/Kalshi.pdf)
- Comparison sites say the 0.07 coefficient is still standard in 2026, peaking at about $1.75 per 100 contracts at a 50¢ price. Some series carry non-standard fees. — [SI](https://www.si.com/prediction-markets/reviews/kalshi-vs-polymarket); [GamblingSite](https://www.gamblingsite.com/prediction-markets/fees/)
- Polymarket US's taker coefficient is reported as either 0.0695 (schedule effective Oct 19, 2026; maximum $1.74 per 100 contracts) or 0.06 (effective July 1, 2026; maximum $1.50). Makers reportedly pay nothing, with rebates at high volume. This is unresolved, and some pages mix in the different international fee schedule. — [PredictionMarketsPicks](https://predictionmarketspicks.com/polymarket-us/fees); [River Markets](https://www.rivermarkets.com/insights/kalshi-vs-polymarket.html)

**Who profits: account-level and wallet-level evidence**
- **Kalshi (Bürgi, Deng & Whelan; Jan 2026 version; data from 2021 to April 2025).**
  - The data covers 313,972 Yes and No contract prices, from 46,282 contracts across 12,403 events.
  - Buyers of contracts costing under 10¢ "lose over 60 percent of their money." Contracts above 50¢ earn a small positive return, and above 70¢ a statistically significant one after fees. For example, a 5¢ contract that wins 3% of the time returns −40% before fees, while a 95¢ contract that wins 98% of the time returns +3.1%.
  - The average per-contract return is about −20%. This is an unweighted average; the money-weighted return before fees is zero.
  - Makers averaged −9.64% per contract and takers −31.46%.
  - There is "some evidence that the bias in prices is diminishing over time." — [PDF](https://www.karlwhelan.com/Papers/Kalshi.pdf); [CEPR DP20631](https://cepr.org/publications/dp20631)
- **Kalshi sports (Moshrefi, arXiv July 15, 2026; about 23 million moneyline trades).**
  - Prices are close to calibrated mid-life but become distorted in the final 10 minutes before expiry, linked to "insurance-demand" buying by traders holding losing positions.
  - Cross-game parlays are priced above the product of their legs, and the overpricing grows with the number of legs. — [arXiv 2607.14430](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14430)
- **Polymarket.**
  - Solidus Labs (reported Apr 29, 2026) studied politics markets from December 2025 to February 2026. 0.55% of profitable maker wallets captured 50% of gains, and 0.26% of winning taker wallets captured nearly the same share (about $8 million of roughly $16 million in profits for each group). About 15% of volume in some markets looked like self-trading or economically neutral activity. Solidus sells surveillance tools to prediction markets, which is a potential bias.
  - In the same article, a London Business School and Yale paper found about 3% of traders drive most price discovery. — [CoinDesk (Apr 29, 2026)](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2026/04/29/a-tiny-group-is-winning-on-polymarket-as-under-1-of-wallets-take-half-the-profits)
  - Telonex studied 46,945 wallets in 15-minute crypto markets over one week. Only 36.8% finished in profit, and the median result was about −$3. — [Telonex](https://telonex.io/research/top-crypto-traders-polymarket-15m)
  - An on-chain study attributed to Thorsten Meyer, widely syndicated and not verified, covered 95 million transactions from April 2024 to December 2025. It found only 0.51% of wallets made profits above $1,000. — [The Idea Magazine (syndicated)](https://theideamagazine.com/business-industry/are-polymarket-trading-bots-actually-profitable-the-math-behind-2026-s-predictio/)
  - Convexly audited leaderboard wallets only and says its results do not apply to the long tail of ordinary users. — [Convexly](https://www.convexly.app/research/polymarket-10k-wallet-study)
  - Profit leaders are "operating in a different league entirely," relying on capital, infrastructure and execution most users lack. — [CoinDesk/Solidus](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2026/04/29/a-tiny-group-is-winning-on-polymarket-as-under-1-of-wallets-take-half-the-profits)

### Inferences
- Fee drag on Kalshi, by my arithmetic from the formula:
  - At 50¢ the fee is 1.75¢, about 3.5% of the money paid. Bürgi et al. state it as 1.75% to 1.77% of the price, apparently measured against the $1 payout.
  - At 10¢ the fee is about 0.63¢, about 6.3% of the money paid.
  - At 90¢ the fee is about 0.63¢, about 0.7% of the money paid.
  - So fees are proportionally heaviest on the cheap longshots where the bias already hurts most.
- Because the market is zero-sum before fees, the gains of skilled makers, arbitrage bots and well-capitalized traders come out of the pockets of less-informed takers. A typical recreational user who buys longshots or parlays and crosses the spread has clearly negative expected value. The only positive-return pocket in the academic data (favorites above 70¢, 2021 to April 2025) was small, and the bias may be shrinking.
- Sports contracts are now the bulk of activity at several venues (for example, about 70% of Robinhood event-contract trading in Q3 2026). Their legal status could change state by state depending on the Supreme Court. A user could see markets shut off in their state, as happened with Polymarket in Nevada.

### Gaps
- No audited, platform-wide share of Kalshi or Polymarket accounts in profit was found. Wallet studies cover specific market types and windows, and one wallet can be many people, or one person can control many wallets.
- I did not find full-year 2025 Kalshi and Polymarket totals from a primary source. October 2026 figures conflict.
- The Polymarket US launch date and current CFTC status are inconsistently reported. RotoWire's timeline conflicts with The Block's data showing Polymarket US volume in 2026.
- A Columbia study on Polymarket wash trading (2025) and other on-chain analyses were not retrieved.
- Reports that the Eighth Circuit also ruled the contracts are not swaps could not be confirmed.

## Q3. Sports betting: legal status, hold, bettor outcomes, limiting of winners, household financial effects, help resources

### Takeaway
Sports betting is legal in 39 states plus DC, and online or mobile betting in about 32 states plus DC as of 2026. In 2025, sportsbooks kept about 10.2% of every dollar wagered nationally ($16.96 billion of revenue on $166.94 billion of handle), up from about 9.2% in 2024. No credible primary data shows what share of bettors win long term. Regulator data shows books limit a small share of accounts (0.64% in Massachusetts), and those limits fall disproportionately on winners. Peer-reviewed and NBER research links legal online betting to lower savings, more overdrafts and credit-card debt among constrained households, a 25% to 30% higher bankruptcy likelihood, and lower credit scores.

### Cited Findings
**Legal status**
- "In 39 states and Washington, D.C., voters and elected leaders have chosen to legalize and regulate sports betting." — [AGA, Sports Event Contracts page (updated about late Oct 2026)](https://www.americangaming.org/sports-event-contracts/)
- About 32 states plus DC offer statewide online sports betting. One guide dates this to May 26, 2026, and another to August 2026, which also counts Puerto Rico. Missouri went live Dec 1, 2025. Wisconsin legalized online betting on Apr 9, 2026, with launch pending. These are secondary trackers. — [Fox Sports](https://www.foxsports.com/stories/betting/where-is-sports-betting-legal); [Tech-Insider (secondary)](https://tech-insider.org/sports-betting/sports-betting-by-state/)

**Hold and house edge**
- 2025 AGA figures: handle $166.94 billion (+11%), revenue $16.96 billion (+22.8%), and state taxes paid $3.71 billion (+32.4%). — [AGA press release (Feb 2026)](https://www.americangaming.org/commercial-gaming-revenue-hits-78-7-billion-in-2025-driving-record-18-1-billion-in-gaming-taxes-nationwide/)
- The AGA's State of the States 2026 puts revenue at $16.89 billion (+22.6%), excluding tribal books and Florida mobile. — [AGA State of the States 2026 (PDF)](https://www.americangaming.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/AGA-State-of-the-States-2026.pdf)
- Implied national hold was about 10.2% in 2025, up from 9.2% in 2024, and the AGA attributes the 2025 revenue gain to that higher hold. Handle fell about 2% in Q1 2026, the first quarterly decline on record. — [Legal Sports Report](https://www.legalsportsreport.com/263300/sportsbooks-make-more-money-from-slower-sports-betting-growth-in-2025/)
- At standard −110 odds, a bettor must win 52.38% of bets to break even. Commonly cited professional win rates are 53% to 55%. This comes from betting-education sites (secondary). — [Boyd's Bets](https://www.boydsbets.com/percentage-profitable-sports-bettors/); [PlusEVBets](https://plusevbets.com/learn/guides/what-percentage-bettors-profitable)

**Bettor outcomes and limiting of winners**
- The figure that only about 2% to 5% (most often "3%") of bettors are long-term winners appears across tipster and affiliate sites with no traceable primary data. Treat it as folklore-grade. A survey cited there found 40% of bettors claim a net gain over 12 months, which is self-reported and short-term. — [Boyd's Bets](https://www.boydsbets.com/percentage-profitable-sports-bettors/); [Nostrabet](https://nostrabet.com/en/news/sports-bettors-think-they-make-money-yet-3-percent-profit/)
- Massachusetts Gaming Commission data from operators (December 2024 to September 2025):
  - Bet limits affected 0.64% of accounts. About 58% of limited accounts were cut to between 1% and 24% of the default maximum bet.
  - A rule effective June 1, 2026 requires books to give a personalized explanation within 48 hours when they limit an account. Generic "business decision" language is not allowed.
  - Coverage reports winners' limits were cut while losing bettors often saw limits raised.
  - Fanatics told regulators nearly half of its limited accounts were net losers at the time they were limited. BetMGM said it limits about 1% of Massachusetts customers. — [Gaming Intelligence](https://www.gamingintelligence.com/sectors/betting/231532-massachusetts-first-to-require-sportsbooks-to-justify-account-limits-on-winning-bettors/); [CDC Gaming Reports](https://cdcgaming.com/massachusetts-proxy-betting-limiting-violations-on-regulatory-agenda/)

**Household financial effects**
- **Baker, Balthrop, Johnson, Kotter & Pisciotta, "Gambling Away Stability" (NBER Working Paper 33108, November 2024; published in Journal of Financial Economics vol. 183, 2026).**
  - The study uses household transaction data and staggered state legalization.
  - Betting participation and frequency rise after legalization. Betting does not displace other gambling or consumption, but it significantly reduces savings and investment.
  - Financially constrained households see higher credit-card debt, less available credit and more frequent overdrafts. — [NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/w33108)
- **Hollenbeck, Larsen & Proserpio, "The Financial Consequences of Legalized Sports Gambling" (SSRN 4903302; first circulated July 2024).**
  - Data covers about 4.38 million adults in the University of California Credit Panel, March 2016 to June 2023.
  - In states with online betting: bankruptcy likelihood rose 25% to 30%, collections rose 8%, and credit-card and auto-loan delinquencies rose about 8% each. Credit scores fell about 2% per the Feb 2026 summary. Retail-only states showed small or insignificant effects. — [UCLA Anderson Review (Feb 4, 2026)](https://anderson-review.ucla.edu/as-states-permitted-online-sports-gambling-citizens-personal-financial-health-suffered)
  - Version differences: 2024 coverage cited about a 1% credit-score decline. A December 2025 SSRN abstract, as summarized by search, reported about 0.7 points for general legal access versus about 12 points for online. Effects are concentrated among young men in lower-income areas. — [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=4903302); [Fortune (July 2024)](https://dc.fortune.com/2024/07/26/online-sports-betting-legalization-loan-delinquencies-bankruptcies-debt-collections-credit-scores)

**Problem-gambling resources**
- The National Council on Problem Gambling (NCPG) adopted 1-800-MY-RESET as the new National Problem Gambling Helpline number in early 2026 (covered Jan 29, 2026). It is free, confidential and available 24/7, with referrals to local help.
  - The older line 1-800-522-4700 remains active, and 1-800-GAMBLER still operates under a separate operator.
  - On Sept 29, 2025, the New Jersey Supreme Court denied NCPG's emergency stay to keep using 1-800-GAMBLER. — [NCPG announcement](https://www.ncpgambling.org/news/1-800-my-reset-announcement/); [NCPG FAQ](https://www.ncpgambling.org/1-800-my-reset-national-problem-gambling-helpline-faq/); [Legal Sports Report](https://www.legalsportsreport.com/252681/ncpg-launches-1-800-my-reset-as-new-problem-gambling-helpline/)
- NCPG's older fact sheet lists 1-800-GAMBLER call, text "800GAM", and chat. This predates the change. — [NCPG fact sheet (2023)](https://www.ncpgambling.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/1-800-GAMBLER-Fact-Sheet.pdf)

### Inferences
- A 10.2% national hold means bettors as a group lost about $10 per $100 wagered in 2025. Part of the rise in hold reflects more parlays and props, which carry higher margins than single −110 bets.
- On a balanced −110/−110 market the book's built-in margin is about 4.5%, by my arithmetic (risk $110 to win $100; break-even 52.38%).
- Account limiting means even a bettor who develops an edge may have their stakes capped. Upside is restricted while downside is not.
- On the keypad, MY-RESET corresponds to 1-800-697-3738. Confirm with NCPG before publishing.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed US study gives the share of online bettors who are long-term winners. The common "3%" figure is untraceable.
- The specific magnitudes in Baker et al. (for example, the percentage decline in net investment) were not retrieved, because the NBER page lists no numbers.
- The current-version Hollenbeck et al. effect sizes were not confirmed because the SSRN page blocked fetching.
- I did not verify state-specific hold rates (for example, New York or Illinois parlay hold) from regulator reports.
- Colorado's reported August 2026 law barring limiting of winners could not be confirmed.
- State self-exclusion program details were not retrieved.

## Q4. Expected value for a typical participant in each, and why

### Takeaway
All three are negative expected value for the typical participant. Options and prediction markets are zero-sum before costs, but spreads, fees and well-documented behavior (buying cheap longshots, short-dated lottery-like options, parlays) make the typical retail result negative. Sports betting is negative by design through the vig and hold (about 10% of handle in 2025), and winners risk being limited. Positive expected value exists mainly for market makers, wholesalers, sportsbooks, and a small minority of skilled, well-capitalized or automated traders.

### Cited Findings
- Options: retail buyers of short-dated options lose on average after spreads. The 0DTE retail proxy lost $241,000 to $350,000 per day, about 60% of it transaction costs. Earnings-period trades lost 5% to 9% on average. — [Beckmeyer et al.](https://wp.lancs.ac.uk/fofi2024/files/2024/04/FoFI-2024-146-Leander-Gayda.pdf); [MIT Sloan](https://mitsloan.mit.edu/ideas-made-to-matter/retail-investors-lose-big-options-markets-research-shows)
- Counterpoint: Cboe customer price-improvement-auction trades held to expiration were not significantly unprofitable from 2020 to 2023, in a strongly rising market. — [Amaya et al. 2025](https://cdn.cboe.com/resources/education/research_publications/Retail_Profitability.pdf)
- Prediction markets: zero money-weighted return before fees. Average per-contract returns were −31.46% for takers and −9.64% for makers. Sub-10¢ contracts lost more than 60%. — [Bürgi, Deng & Whelan (Jan 2026)](https://www.karlwhelan.com/Papers/Kalshi.pdf)
- Polymarket profits are highly concentrated (under 1% of wallets take about half of profits in politics markets). — [CoinDesk/Solidus](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2026/04/29/a-tiny-group-is-winning-on-polymarket-as-under-1-of-wallets-take-half-the-profits)
- Sports betting: national hold of about 10.2% in 2025. — [Legal Sports Report](https://www.legalsportsreport.com/263300/sportsbooks-make-more-money-from-slower-sports-betting-growth-in-2025/)
- Massachusetts books limited 0.64% of accounts, with limits skewed toward winners. — [Gaming Intelligence](https://www.gamingintelligence.com/sectors/betting/231532-massachusetts-first-to-require-sportsbooks-to-justify-account-limits-on-winning-bettors/)

### Inferences
- Rough "cost per $100 put at risk" (not directly comparable across products):
  - Sports betting: about $10 per $100 wagered on average (2025 hold), about $4.50 on a single balanced −110 bet, and more on parlays.
  - Kalshi-type markets: up to about $3.50 per $100 in fees on 50¢ contracts and about $6.30 per $100 on 10¢ contracts, before any pricing bias. The bias adds large further losses on longshots.
  - 0DTE and short-dated options: about $3 per $100 of premium each time the retail-proxy trader crosses an effective spread of about 6%, roughly double for a round trip and for tiny-premium trades.
- Why this matters for someone seeking side income: these products have high variance, so some individuals will win in any given month or year. The averages and the concentration of profits indicate that the typical participant should expect to lose money over time, with costs compounding as trading frequency rises. The only consistently positive pockets in the evidence are the house (sportsbooks), liquidity providers (market makers, wholesalers, prediction-market makers and bots), and a small skilled minority whose edge is often capped (sportsbook limits) or depends on capital and infrastructure (Polymarket top wallets).

### Gaps
- No study directly compares the same individuals' outcomes across options, prediction markets and sportsbooks.
- No account-level US data source covers what share of retail options traders are profitable over multiple years.
