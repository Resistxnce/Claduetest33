Scope note for the report writer: Every rate and limit below has a date. "Secondary" means I could not open the primary document and relied on a reputable summary. Where sources conflict, both are shown. Today's date is 2026-11-11. Two live facts shape the whole picture. ## 1. Broad index funds/ETFs (S&P 500, total US market, total world) in a taxable brokerage account: long-run returns, worst drawdowns and recovery times, costs, and how fast you can get cash

### Takeaway
- **Long-run returns:** US stocks have returned about 10% a year nominal (S&P 500 with dividends, 1928-2025) and about 6.6% a year after inflation (US equities, 1900-2025). Big forecasters expect less for the next decade: J.P. Morgan's Oct 2026 forecast is 7.5% a year for US large caps.
- **Crashes:** Drops of 34-57% have happened in living memory. Recovery took anywhere from about 4 months (2025) to about 7 years (2000-02, price basis), and about 25 years after 1929.
- **Cost and access:** Core index ETFs cost about 0.03-0.07% a year. Trades settle the next business day (T+1), so the money is liquid but its value is not guaranteed.

### Inferences
- For money the reader may need within roughly 1-3 years, stock index funds are a poor fit. They are *liquid* (cash in one business day) but not *stable*. Since 2000, price recoveries have taken anywhere from about 4 months to about 7 years, and a forced sale near a bottom locks in a 20-50% loss. This suggests a split: an emergency/near-term bucket in cash-like assets, and a long-term bucket in index funds.
- Expected returns are lower than recent history. 2016-2025 returned about 14.7% a year. History says about 10% nominal or 6.6% real, and J.P. Morgan forecasts 7.5% nominal. Planning on 6-8% nominal is more prudent than extrapolating the last decade.
- Fees: a 0.03% fund costs $3 a year per $10,000. The 0.40% average equity mutual fund costs $40. Cost differences compound but are small compared with behavior (see Q2).
- After a sale settles (T+1), moving cash from the brokerage to a bank usually takes an additional 1-3 business days. This is general practice and was not verified here. Plan on about a week end-to-end for money in a brokerage account.

## 2. Active vs index (latest SPIVA US) and the investor "behavior gap" (latest Morningstar Mind the Gap)

### Takeaway
- **Active funds:** Over long periods, about 83-93% of active US large-cap funds trailed the S&P 500 (SPIVA Mid-Year 2026, data to June 30, 2026). Underperformance rises with time, and many funds disappear: only about 37% of large-cap funds survived 20 years.
- **Investor behavior:** Separately, the average dollar in US funds earned 1.2 points a year *less* than the funds themselves, because of buy/sell timing (Morningstar 2026). US equity index fund investors were the exception, with almost no gap (0.1 pt).

### Inferences
- For a non-expert, the evidence points to a simple strategy: buy and hold broad, low-cost index funds with automatic contributions. That beats most professional stock-pickers over 10-20 years (SPIVA) and is the style where investors captured nearly all of the fund's return (US equity index funds: 0.1-point gap).
- The behavior gap is the "cost" of trading more and reacting to volatility. Day trading is the extreme version of the behaviors Morningstar associates with the widest gaps: high-volatility products, frequent ad-hoc transactions, and crypto/leveraged ETFs.

- **Limits:** $10,000 per SSN per calendar year, electronic only since Jan 1, 2025. Minimum purchase $25.
- **Access:** Cannot be cashed for **12 months**. Cashing before 5 years **forfeits the last 3 months of interest**.
- **Tax:** Federal tax can be deferred until redemption. Exempt from state and local tax. There is a possible education exclusion.
- Prior rate (May-Oct 2026): 4.26% with a 0.90% fixed rate. — [Tipswatch](https://tipswatch.com/2026/10/30/treasury-sets-i-bonds-fixed-rate-of-1-40-composite-rate-rises-to-5-51/)

### Inferences
- **Real returns on cash are thin.** With CPI at 3.7%, a 4.3% HYSA earns about 0.6% before tax, and that interest is taxed as ordinary income at the federal and state level. In a 22% federal bracket, its after-tax real return is about zero or negative. Cash is for safety and access, not growth.
- **T-bills vs HYSAs.** T-bills (about 4.2-4.5% for 3-12 months) beat most HYSAs and are state-tax-free, which helps more in high-tax states. They need a brokerage or TreasuryDirect account and are slightly less convenient. Treasury money market funds are the near-equivalent with daily liquidity.
- **I bonds for "not needed this year" money.** A 1.40% real rate locked for up to 30 years, plus inflation, is unusually good. The trade-offs are the 12-month lockup, the 3-month interest penalty before 5 years, and the $10k/yr cap. They suit money that won't be needed for at least a year, not an emergency fund the reader may need next month.
- **Rates may still change.** Markets are pricing possible further hikes; the 52-week bill yield is above the 4-week. HYSA and money fund yields float with the Fed, while a T-bill or I bond fixes its rate for its term.

### Gaps
- No **government-only** money market fund average 7-day yield (for example, the Crane Government Institutional average) for November 2026 was found. The Crane 100 (3.75%) includes prime funds.
- HYSA top rates vary by tracker and date: about 4.0-4.35% unconditional, up to 4.70-5% with conditions or promos. Bank pages were not checked directly.
- The October 2026 12-month CPI rate was not published by BLS (see above).

## 4. Roth IRA: limits, access rules, earned-income requirement, custodial Roth IRAs

### Takeaway
- **Limits:** 2026 limit is **$7,500** (+$1,100 catch-up at 50+). Income phase-out: $153k-$168k single, $242k-$252k married filing jointly. **2027 limits have not been announced as of Nov 11, 2026**, and projections disagree on the IRA limit.
- **Access:** Roth IRA *contributions* come out first and can be withdrawn at any time without tax or the 10% penalty. *Earnings* generally need age 59½ plus 5 years. This makes a Roth a flexible "long-term but reachable" account.
- **Earned income:** You need earned income at least equal to the contribution. That includes minors with jobs (custodial Roth IRAs).

### Cited Findings
- **2026 IRA limits (IRS IR-2025-111, Nov 13, 2025)** — [IRS](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/401k-limit-increases-to-24500-for-2026-ira-limit-increases-to-7500)
  - IRA contribution limit: **$7,500**, up from $7,000. Age 50+ catch-up: $1,100.
  - Roth IRA income phase-out:
    - Single / head of household: **$153,000-$168,000**.
    - Married filing jointly: **$242,000-$252,000**.
    - Married filing separately: $0-$10,000.
  - Saver's Credit income limits: $40,250 single, $60,375 head of household, $80,500 married filing jointly.
- **2027:** No official IRS announcement as of the latest sources. The 2026 limits were announced Nov 13, 2025, and 2027 figures were expected in early-to-mid November 2026. — [401(k) Specialist, Oct 20, 2026](https://401kspecialistmag.com/2027-401k-contribution-limits-all-but-official-25500-expected/)
  - Projections for the 2027 IRA limit conflict: some keep it at **$7,500**, others project **$8,000**.
  - Projected 2027 Roth phase-outs are about $157k-$158k to $172k-$173k (single) and about $248k-$250k to $258k-$260k (married filing jointly).
  - [UStax.tools projection](https://ustax.tools/retirement-contribution-limits-2027/); [The Finance Buff (updated Oct 14, 2026)](https://thefinancebuff.com/401k-403b-ira-contribution-limits.html) (unofficial; conflicting)
- **Withdrawal ordering rules** (IRS Publication 590-B, for 2025 returns) — [IRS Pub 590-B](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p590b)
  - Roth distributions come out in a set order: **regular contributions first**, then conversion/rollover contributions (first-in, first-out), then earnings.
  - Distributions that are a return of regular contributions are not included in gross income.
  - The 10% additional tax applies to "the taxable part" of non-qualified distributions, so returned contributions are not hit.
- **Qualified distribution:** Made after a 5-year period starting with the first tax year a Roth contribution was made, *and* on or after age 59½, on disability, at death, or as a first-home distribution ($10,000 lifetime). — [IRS Pub 590-B](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p590b)
- **Exceptions to the 10% early-distribution tax** (IRS page updated Dec 11, 2025) — [IRS: Exceptions to tax on early distributions](https://www.irs.gov/retirement-plans/plan-participant-employee/retirement-topics-exceptions-to-tax-on-early-distributions)

  | Exception | Limit | 401(k) | IRA |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Emergency personal expense | Lesser of $1,000 or vested balance over $1,000; once a year | Yes | Yes |
  | Birth or adoption | $5,000 per child | Yes | Yes |
  | First-time homebuyer | $10,000 | No | Yes |
  | Higher education | Qualified expenses | No | Yes |
  | Unreimbursed medical | Above 7.5% of AGI | Yes | Yes |
  | Disability, domestic abuse, others | Various | Yes | Yes |
  | Separation from service at age 55+ | n/a | Yes | No |

- **Earned income and custodial Roth IRAs** — [NerdWallet, custodial Roth IRA](https://www.nerdwallet.com/retirement/learn/why-your-kid-needs-a-roth-ira); [Vanguard custodial IRA](https://investor.vanguard.com/accounts-plans/iras/custodial-ira)
  - Contributions are limited to the lesser of earned income or the annual limit ($7,500 in 2026).
  - Minors can have a **custodial Roth IRA** if they have earned income; there is no minimum age.
  - Contributions can be withdrawn anytime. Earnings are generally locked until 59½ unless an exception applies.
- Fidelity (Sept 10, 2026): a custodial Roth IRA "requires the child's earned income," and contributions can be withdrawn anytime. — [Fidelity](https://www.fidelity.com/learning-center/personal-finance/trump-accounts)

### Inferences
- For someone who wants growth but also wants access, a Roth IRA invested in a broad index fund works as a "backstop emergency fund." Contributions (not gains) can come out tax- and penalty-free. Once withdrawn, though, that contribution room is lost: the annual limit cannot be refilled later, aside from 60-day rollover rules not covered here.
- A worker earning $50k-$100k is well under the Roth income limits, so the main constraint is the $7,500 cap.

### Gaps
- Official 2027 IRA limit and Roth phase-outs: not yet published as of Nov 11, 2026. Check IRS.gov.
- I did not pull IRS Pub 590-A's exact wording on the compensation requirement. The source used was secondary (NerdWallet, Fidelity).

## 5. 401(k): limits, employer match, how many miss the match, loans and hardship withdrawals

### Takeaway
- **Limits:** The 2026 employee limit is **$24,500** (+$8,000 at 50+; $11,250 at ages 60-63). 2027 is projected at **$25,500**, not yet official.
- **Match:** The typical promised match is about 4-4.7% of pay. The most common formula is 50 cents per dollar on the first 6%, which is an *instant* 50% return on the matched dollars. Yet about **42%** of workers in Vanguard data did not get the full match: 16% weren't contributing and 26% were below the cap.
- **Access:** 401(k) money can be reached via loans (up to the lesser of $50k or 50% of the vested balance, repaid within 5 years) or hardship withdrawals, which are taxable and can carry the 10% penalty. It is less accessible than a Roth IRA's contributions.

### Cited Findings
- **2026 limits (IRS, Nov 13, 2025)** — [IRS](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/401k-limit-increases-to-24500-for-2026-ira-limit-increases-to-7500)
  - Elective deferral: **$24,500** (from $23,500).
  - Age 50+ catch-up: $8,000, for a $32,500 total.
  - Ages 60-63 catch-up: $11,250.
  - SIMPLE: $17,000.
- **2027 projections (Milliman final estimates, Oct 15, 2026; matching Marsh)** — [Milliman](https://www.milliman.com/en/insight/2027-irs-limits-forecast-final-estimates); [401(k) Specialist, Oct 20, 2026](https://401kspecialistmag.com/2027-401k-contribution-limits-all-but-official-25500-expected/)
  - These are **not official**; the IRS was expected to announce in early-to-mid Nov 2026.
  - Deferral: **$25,500**. Age 50+ catch-up: $8,500. Ages 60-63 catch-up: $11,750.
  - Total annual additions: $75,000.
  - Wage threshold for the mandatory Roth catch-up: $150,000 → $155,000.
  - The 2027 HSA limits are already official: $4,500 self-only and $9,000 family.
- **Match formulas** (Vanguard How America Saves 2026, published June 2026, 2025 data) — [Vanguard HAS 2026](https://workplace.vanguard.com/content/dam/inst/iig-transformation/has/2026/pdf/HowAmericaSaves2026.pdf)
  - Most common formula: **$0.50 per $1 on the first 6% of pay**, used by 12% of matching plans.
  - Other common formulas:
    - $1 on the first 3% + $0.50 on the next 2%: 9%.
    - 100% on the first 6%: 9%.
    - 100% on the first 5%: 7%.
    - 100% on the first 4%: 7%.
  - Single-tier formulas: 69% of plans. Multi-tier: 25%. Dollar-capped: 5%.
  - Promised match: average **4.7% of pay**, median **4.0%**.
  - Deferral needed to get the full match: average **6.4%** of pay, median 6.0%.
- **Other HAS 2026 plan statistics (2025)** — [Vanguard HAS 2026](https://workplace.vanguard.com/content/dam/inst/iig-transformation/has/2026/pdf/HowAmericaSaves2026.pdf)
  - 96% of plans make some employer contribution; 61% auto-enroll.
  - Participation: 94% with auto-enrollment vs 64% voluntary; 83% overall (participant-weighted).
  - Average deferral 7.6% (median 6.6%). Total savings rate including the employer averages 12.1%.
  - 14% hit the $23,500 limit in 2025.
  - Account balances: average $167,970; median $44,115.
- **Workers missing the full match** (Vanguard, "Better match formulas for 401(k) plans," July 2026; 2023 administrative data) — [Vanguard research PDF](https://corporate.vanguard.com/content/dam/corp/research/pdf/better_match_formulas_401k_plans.pdf); [Vanguard summary, Aug 4, 2026](https://corporate.vanguard.com/content/corporatesite/us/en/corp/articles/new-401k-match-formulas-can-unlock-retirement-gains.html)
  - How workers contributed relative to the match cap: **16%** not contributing, **26%** below the cap, 16% at the cap, 43% above the cap. So about **42% did not receive the full match**.
  - Share of workers who capture the full match, by annual income:

    | Income | Share capturing the full match |
    |---|---|
    | Under $30k | 22% |
    | $30-60k | 52% |
    | $60-90k | 67% |
    | $90-120k | 71% |
    | $120-150k | 76% |
    | $150-180k | 80% |
    | Over $180k | 80% |

  - "Even among workers earning $150,000 or more, roughly 20% do not save enough to capture the full match."
- An older Vanguard figure (How America Saves 2019) put the share not getting the full match at about one-third. — [Money.com](https://money.com/one-third-of-workers-are-making-a-big-mistake-with-their-401k) (older, labeled 2019)
- A 2026 Vanguard piece reportedly finds that about 30% of employees who prepay mortgage, auto or student loans miss part of their match, about $1,100 a year on average. — [Vanguard, Sept 2, 2026](https://workplace.vanguard.com/insights-and-research/perspective/help-participants-avoid-costly-money-mistakes.html) (seen only in a search summary; not opened)
- **Loans** (IRS FAQ, updated Feb 26, 2026) — [IRS loans FAQ](https://www.irs.gov/retirement-plans/retirement-plans-faqs-regarding-loans)
  - Maximum: the lesser of $50,000 or the greater of $10,000 or 50% of the vested balance.
  - Repayment: within 5 years, longer for a principal-residence loan. Payments must be substantially level, at least quarterly.
  - Default: a defaulted loan becomes a taxable "deemed distribution," with early-distribution tax possible.
  - Leaving the job: the plan may offset the unpaid loan against the balance. That can be rolled over until the tax-return due date, including extensions.
- **Loan and hardship usage (Vanguard HAS 2026, 2025 data)** — [Vanguard HAS 2026](https://workplace.vanguard.com/content/dam/inst/iig-transformation/has/2026/pdf/HowAmericaSaves2026.pdf)
  - 82% of plans offer loans. **13%** of participants had a loan outstanding; the average loan balance was about $11,000.
  - 94% of plans offer hardship withdrawals. **6%** of participants took one in 2025, up from 5% in 2024 and 2% in 2021.
- The IRS's list of exceptions to the 10% additional tax does not include "hardship" as such. 401(k)-eligible exceptions include the $1,000/yr emergency personal expense, $5,000 birth/adoption, separation from service at 55+, and medical costs above 7.5% of AGI. — [IRS exceptions page](https://www.irs.gov/retirement-plans/plan-participant-employee/retirement-topics-exceptions-to-tax-on-early-distributions)

### Inferences
- **The "instant return" math.** With a 50%-on-6% match, a worker earning $50,000 who contributes 6% ($3,000) gets $1,500 free: an immediate 50% return before any market gain, plus a tax deduction if the contribution is pre-tax. With a 100% match (for example, 100% on the first 4%), the immediate return is 100%. No investment strategy, and certainly not day trading, reliably beats that. Caveat: vesting schedules can claw back unvested match if the worker leaves early. Vesting was not researched here.
- **Priority order for a typical worker.** Getting the full match is usually the first priority after a small emergency fund and paying off high-rate debt. That is because 401(k) money is harder to access: loans and hardship withdrawals have costs, taxes and the possible 10% penalty.

### Gaps
- Official 2027 401(k) limits: pending at the IRS as of Nov 11, 2026.
- Vesting schedule statistics and how quickly match money becomes the employee's were not collected.
- How America Saves 2026 does not itself publish a "share not getting the full match" figure. The 42% comes from Vanguard's July 2026 paper using 2023 data.

## 6. Taxes in a taxable brokerage account (capital gains, dividends, wash sales, day trading)

### Takeaway
- **Gains and dividends:** Gains on investments held more than one year are taxed at 0%, 15% or 20%. In 2026, the 0% rate applies up to $49,450 of taxable income (single) or $98,900 (married filing jointly). Gains held one year or less, which includes all day-trading gains, are taxed as ordinary income at up to 37%. Qualified dividends get the lower long-term rates.
- **Losses:** Net losses offset gains and up to $3,000 of ordinary income per year. The wash-sale rule blocks a loss if you rebuy the same or a substantially identical security within 30 days before or after the sale.

### Cited Findings
- **Holding period:** More than one year is long-term. "Net short-term capital gains are subject to taxation as ordinary income at graduated tax rates." — [IRS Topic 409 (updated Nov 9, 2026)](https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc409)
- **Other Topic 409 rules** — [IRS Topic 409](https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc409)
  - Collectibles are taxed at up to 28%.
  - Net capital losses are deductible up to $3,000 a year ($1,500 married filing separately); the excess carries forward.
  - The net investment income tax may apply to "significant investment income."
- **2026 long-term capital gains thresholds** (Rev. Proc. 2025-32) — [Kiplinger](https://www.kiplinger.com/taxes/irs-updates-capital-gains-tax-thresholds); [Experian](https://www.experian.com/blogs/ask-experian/long-term-capital-gains-tax-rates/) (secondary, citing Rev. Proc. 2025-32)

  | Rate | Single (taxable income) | Married filing jointly (taxable income) |
  |---|---|---|
  | 0% | Up to **$49,450** | Up to **$98,900** |
  | 15% | Up to $545,500 | Up to $613,700 |
  | 20% | Above that | Above that |

  - For 2025 (per IRS Topic 409), the single 0%/15% cutoffs were $48,350 and $533,400. — [IRS Topic 409](https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc409)
- **2026 standard deduction and ordinary brackets** (IRS IR-2025-103, Oct 9, 2025; Rev. Proc. 2025-32) — [IRS](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/irs-releases-tax-inflation-adjustments-for-tax-year-2026-including-amendments-from-the-one-big-beautiful-bill)
  - Standard deduction: **$16,100** single, **$32,200** married filing jointly, $24,150 head of household.

  | Rate | Single: taxable income over | Married filing jointly: taxable income over |
  |---|---|---|
  | 10% | $0 | $0 |
  | 12% | $12,400 | $24,800 |
  | 22% | $50,400 | $100,800 |
  | 24% | $105,700 | $211,400 |
  | 32% | $201,775 | $403,550 |
  | 35% | $256,225 | $512,450 |
  | 37% | $640,600 | $768,700 |

  - Because the long-term thresholds apply to *taxable* income, a single filer could have about $65,550 of gross income ($49,450 + $16,100) and still be in the 0% long-term gains bracket. — [Kiplinger](https://www.kiplinger.com/taxes/irs-updates-capital-gains-tax-thresholds) (secondary)
- **Dividends:** "Qualified dividends are those ordinary dividends that qualify to be taxed at lower capital gain rates"; other taxable dividends are ordinary income. — [IRS Topic 404 (updated Oct 8, 2026)](https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc404)
- **Wash sales** — [Kiplinger](https://www.kiplinger.com/taxes/604947/wash-sale-rule); [J.P. Morgan](https://www.jpmorgan.com/insights/wealth-planning/taxes/wash-sale-rule-things-to-know); [NerdWallet](https://www.nerdwallet.com/investing/learn/wash-sale) (secondary, citing IRS Pub 550)
  - A wash sale is a sale at a loss with a purchase of substantially identical stock or securities within 30 days before or after. That makes a 61-day window, counted in calendar days.
  - The disallowed loss is added to the replacement shares' cost basis, so it is deferred, not lost.
  - The rule can be triggered by purchases by a spouse, or in your own IRA.

### Inferences
- **Day trading vs buy-and-hold.** Every profitable day trade is a short-term gain taxed at the trader's full marginal rate: 22-24% federal for many workers, plus state tax. A buy-and-hold index investor typically defers gains for years and then pays 0% or 15%. For a single worker with $40k-$60k of taxable income, part or all of long-term gains may be taxed at **0%** federally. Frequent traders also trip wash-sale rules easily, which defers losses they hoped to deduct.
- **Index ETFs in a taxable account are tax-efficient.** Low turnover means few capital gains distributions, and selling is the investor's choice. Mechanisms behind ETF tax efficiency were not researched here.

### Gaps
- The net investment income tax thresholds (statutory $200k single / $250k MFJ, not indexed) were not re-verified this session.
- The qualified-dividend holding period (more than 60 days in a 121-day window) was not verified from IRS text.
- Head-of-household long-term gains thresholds for 2026 were not verified.
- State taxes were not covered.

## 7. Paying off high-interest debt as a guaranteed "return"

### Takeaway
- **Card rates:** The average credit card interest rate on accounts that are actually charged interest was **22.36%** in Q3 2026. The rate across all card accounts was 21.19% (Federal Reserve G.19, released Nov 6, 2026).
- **Why it matters:** Paying off a card at about 22% is a risk-free, tax-free 22% "return". That is roughly double the long-run stock market average, and no index fund or trading strategy reliably beats it.

### Cited Findings
- **Fed G.19 credit card rates** (commercial banks; release of Nov 6, 2026) — [Federal Reserve G.19](https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/g19/current/default.htm)

  | Measure | Q3 2025 | Q4 2025 | Q1 2026 | Q2 2026 | Q3 2026 (preliminary) |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | All accounts | 21.39% | 20.97% | 21.00% | 20.94% | **21.19%** |
  | Accounts assessed interest | 22.83% | 22.30% | 21.52% | 22.15% | **22.36%** |

  - Same release, Q3 2026: 24-month personal loan rate 11.90%; 60-month new car loan rate 7.54%.
- For comparison, the long-run S&P 500 return was about 10.0% a year nominal (1928-2025), and J.P. Morgan expects 7.5% a year for US large caps over 10-15 years. — [Damodaran](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/New_Home_Page/datafile/histretSP.html); [J.P. Morgan LTCMA 2027](https://am.jpmorgan.com/us/en/asset-management/institutional/about-us/media/press-releases/jp-morgan-releases-2027-long-term-capital-market-assumptions/)

### Inferences
- **A worked example.** A $5,000 card balance at 22.36% costs about $1,100 a year in interest. Paying it off "earns" that amount with certainty. The same money in an index fund would be expected to earn about $375-$500 a year (at 7.5-10%), with the risk of losses.
- **Suggested order:**
  1. Small emergency cushion.
  2. Get the full 401(k) match (a 50-100% instant return).
  3. Pay off cards and other high-rate debt.
  4. Then invest.
- **Exception: lower-rate loans.** Lower-rate debt, such as a 7.5% car loan, is closer to a toss-up against expected stock returns.

### Gaps
- Bank-specific APRs for new cards and the Fed's "interest rate on all accounts" methodology details were not collected. G.19 measures commercial bank plans only.

## 8. If the person is under 18: UTMA/UGMA, custodial Roth IRA, and "Trump accounts"

### Takeaway
- **Earned income:** A minor *with a job* can use a custodial Roth IRA. This is often the best option because contributions stay withdrawable anytime.
- **Custodial brokerage:** UTMA/UGMA accounts have no contribution cap. The money is irrevocably the child's, and investment income above $2,700 (2026) is taxed at the parent's rate (kiddie tax).
- **Trump accounts:** Created by the 2025 tax law, they **opened July 4, 2026**. They cap contributions at $5,000 a year and allow only low-cost US stock index funds, and the money is **locked until 18**. That makes them a long-term account, not accessible side money.

### Cited Findings
- **Trump accounts — launch, structure and eligibility** — [Fidelity, Sept 10, 2026](https://www.fidelity.com/learning-center/personal-finance/trump-accounts); [CNBC, July 1, 2026](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/01/trump-accounts-launch-july-4.html); [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/trump-account-kids-child-open-july-4/)
  - They became available **July 4, 2026**, under 2025 federal law.
  - They are traditional IRAs for minors, owned by the child with an adult acting until 18, and initially administered by Treasury.
  - Eligibility: children under 18 with an SSN, one funded account per child.
  - $1,000 federal seed: for US citizens born **Jan 1, 2025 - Dec 31, 2028**, with an election filed.
- **Trump accounts — contributions** — [Fidelity](https://www.fidelity.com/learning-center/personal-finance/trump-accounts)
  - Limit: **$5,000 a year per child** from all sources, indexed after 2027. The government seed and "qualified general contributions" from governments and charities don't count toward it.
  - Employers can contribute up to **$2,500 a year** per employee (indexed), which counts toward the $5,000.
  - Individual after-tax contributions are not deductible but are not taxed on withdrawal. Earnings, employer money and the seed are taxed when withdrawn, and the kiddie tax may apply.
- **Trump accounts — investments** — [Fidelity](https://www.fidelity.com/learning-center/personal-finance/trump-accounts)
  - Only mutual funds or ETFs tracking the S&P 500 or another qualifying US equity index (at least 90% US).
  - No leverage. Expense ratio no higher than **0.10%**.
- **Trump accounts — access** — [Fidelity](https://www.fidelity.com/learning-center/personal-finance/trump-accounts); [Jackson Hewitt](https://www.jacksonhewitt.com/tax-help/tax-tips-topics/personal-finance-savings/trump-account-for-kids/)
  - **No withdrawals before 18**, except limited rollovers.
  - After 18, traditional IRA rules generally apply: taxable, with a 10% penalty before 59½ unless an exception applies.
  - Sources differ on whether access starts Jan 1 of the year the child turns 18 or at the 18th birthday.
- **Trump accounts — opening:** Use IRS **Form 4547** or the online tool at trumpaccounts.gov. Treasury then sends activation instructions. Rollovers to private financial institutions come later, pending IRS guidance. — [Fidelity](https://www.fidelity.com/learning-center/personal-finance/trump-accounts)
- **Trump accounts — private pledges and enrollment**
  - Michael and Susan Dell pledged $6.25 billion for $250 deposits for some children age 10 or under. — [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/trump-accounts-kids-under-10-121317737.html)
  - Several companies (for example BlackRock, Robinhood, Uber) announced matches. — [Robinhood newsroom, Jul 6, 2026](https://robinhood.com/newsroom/trump-accounts-officially-launch/)
  - Enrollment figures conflict: more than 4 million children (blog citing the IRS) vs "six million Americans" elsewhere. — [The Caucus Blog, July 2026](https://www.thecaucusblog.com/2026/07/you-can-kickstart-your-babys-long-term.html) (secondary; conflicting)
- **UTMA/UGMA:** No annual contribution limit (gift-tax rules apply), the funds must benefit the child, and growth is subject to the kiddie tax. — [Fidelity comparison table](https://www.fidelity.com/learning-center/personal-finance/trump-accounts)
- **Kiddie tax 2026** — [SmartAsset](https://smartasset.com/taxes/kiddie-tax); [Doeren](https://www.doeren.com/viewpoint/what-to-know-about-the-kiddie-tax-for-2026-planning) (secondary)
  - Threshold: **$2,700**, unchanged from 2025.
  - The first $1,350 of unearned income is tax-free, the next $1,350 is taxed at the child's rate, and amounts above $2,700 are taxed at the parent's rate.
  - It applies to children 18 and under and full-time students 19-23.
- **Custodial Roth IRA:** Requires the minor's earned income. Contributions are capped at the lesser of earned income or $7,500 (2026). Contributions can be withdrawn anytime, while earnings are generally locked until 59½. — [NerdWallet](https://www.nerdwallet.com/retirement/learn/why-your-kid-needs-a-roth-ira); [Vanguard custodial IRA](https://investor.vanguard.com/accounts-plans/iras/custodial-ira)

### Inferences
- **A teen with a job:** The custodial Roth IRA dominates for flexible long-term money. It has tax-free growth, contributions are accessible, and it has a broader investment menu than a Trump account. A Trump account is worth opening mainly to capture "free money": the $1,000 seed for 2025-2028 births, the Dell $250 for eligible younger kids, or employer contributions. It does not fit "side money I might need."
- **Custodial (UTMA/UGMA) brokerage accounts** are accessible for the child's benefit. But the assets legally become the child's at the state's age of majority, and they can affect financial aid. Financial-aid treatment was not researched.

### Gaps
- UTMA/UGMA termination ages by state (commonly 18 or 21) were not verified.
- Financial-aid treatment of these accounts was not researched.
- Primary IRS/Treasury text for Trump accounts was not retrieved. The IRS FAQ URL returned 404 and the CRS report returned 403, so the details rest on Fidelity and news coverage.
- Exact rules for when withdrawals become available at 18 conflict between sources.

## 9. What fixed monthly investments could grow to (illustration and range of outcomes)

### Takeaway
- **Projected values:** At a 7% average annual return, $500 a month grows to about $86k in 10 years, $254k in 20 and $585k in 30, on $60k, $120k and $180k contributed. At history's roughly 10% nominal, the 30-year figure is about $1.03M.
- **Range of outcomes:** Real-world results vary widely. $500 a month in the S&P 500 from 2000 through 2009 ended around $63k on $60k invested. The same plan in 2010-2019 ended around $122k.

### Cited Findings
- **Inputs used for the illustration:**
  - S&P 500 with dividends: about 10.0% a year nominal, 1928-2025. — [Damodaran (Jan 2026)](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/New_Home_Page/datafile/histretSP.html)
  - US equities: 9.8% nominal and 6.6% real a year, 1900-2025. — [UBS Yearbook 2026](https://www.ubs.com/content/dam/assets/wm/static/cio/documents/giry2026-summary-public.pdf)

  - Cash and T-bills at about 4% now, and 3.4% nominal / 0.5% real historically. — [US Treasury bill rates, Nov 10, 2026](https://home.treasury.gov/resource-center/data-chart-center/interest-rates/TextView?type=daily_treasury_bill_rates&field_tdr_date_value_month=202611); [UBS Yearbook](https://www.ubs.com/content/dam/assets/wm/static/cio/documents/giry2026-summary-public.pdf)

- **Timing risk.** Someone in the 2000-2009 plan who needed the money at the March 2009 low would have been well *below* their contributions. Monthly investing smooths entry prices but does not remove the risk of needing money during a drawdown.
- **Inflation.** At about 3% inflation, $1 in 30 years buys roughly what $0.41 buys today. This is why the real (6-7%) columns are the better planning guide for stocks.
- **The match.** For 401(k) savers, a 50% match on the same contributions raises every figure by 50% from day one. For example, $500 a month plus a $250 match is equivalent to $750 a month in the table.
