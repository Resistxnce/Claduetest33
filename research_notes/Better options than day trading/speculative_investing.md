# Speculative investing for US retail investors: crypto, leveraged/inverse ETFs and individual stock picking (evidence as of 2026-11-11)

Scope: US retail perspective. Every figure carries its date; findings older than 2025 are marked "(older)". Some sources could not be opened directly: Morningstar and investor.gov returned 403 errors, and BIS PDFs were blocked by a bot check. Figures from those sources come from search-result excerpts or from secondary coverage and are flagged where used. The FBI IC3 2025 report and the J.P. ## 1. Crypto: bitcoin and ether returns and drawdowns, price context, spot ETFs vs. holding coins, retail outcomes, fraud, altcoins and memecoins

### Takeaway
Bitcoin has made huge long-run gains for early holders who stayed in, but it falls 50-85% repeatedly. On 11 Nov 2026 it traded near $73,900, about 41% below its 6 Oct 2025 record (~$126,200) and about 29% below its price a year earlier. Ether (~$2,055) is about 58% below its Aug 2025 record. The best measurement of actual retail users (BIS, data 2015-2022) found that most crypto-app users lost money on bitcoin. Americans reported $11.4B of crypto-linked fraud losses in 2025 (FBI). Most new tokens die: more than half of tokens tracked since 2021 have stopped trading, and about 98.6% of pump.fun tokens showed rug-pull or pump-and-dump traits.

#### Evidence on retail crypto outcomes
- BIS Bulletin No 69, "Crypto shocks and retail losses" (20 Feb 2023; older; data Aug 2015-Dec 2022):
  - "a majority of crypto app users in nearly all economies made losses on their bitcoin holdings."
  - After the Terra/Luna and FTX collapses, large and sophisticated investors sold while smaller retail investors bought.
  - Source: [BIS](https://www.bis.org/publ/bisbull69.htm)
- The bulletin's detailed numbers, as reported by CoinDesk:
  - The data come from crypto exchange apps in 95 countries.
  - "The median investor would have lost $431 by December 2022," almost half of the $900 they had put in since downloading the app.
  - Under a monthly-investing assumption, "over four fifths of users would have lost money."
  - Shares were higher in Brazil, India, Pakistan, Thailand and Turkey.
  - "larger investors were able to sell their assets to smaller ones before the steep price decline."
  - Source: [CoinDesk via Yahoo Finance, 21 Feb 2023](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/retail-crypto-investors-emerging-economies-162932915.html)
- Companion BIS working paper (Nov 2022; older):
  - Bloomberg reported that 73%-81% of retail bitcoin investors had probably lost money, depending on the assumption — [Bloomberg, 16 Nov 2022](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2022-11-16/vast-majority-of-retail-investors-in-bitcoin-lost-money-bis-says); [Bloomberg Tax: "About 75% of Retail Buyers of Bitcoin Lost Money"](https://news.bloombergtax.com/crypto/about-75-of-retail-buyers-of-bitcoin-lost-money-bis-study-says)
  - Search excerpts of the paper say 73% of users downloaded their app when bitcoin was above $20,000, and "over 75% of users would have lost money." I could not verify this; the PDF was blocked — [BIS WP 1049](https://www.bis.org/publ/work1049.pdf)
  - Commentators note a weakness: the method assumes app downloads mean purchases.
- Newer evidence is thin. A 2025-2026 search found no peer-reviewed study measuring the share of retail crypto investors who are ahead. Items found were commercial surveys or preprints; see Gaps.

### Inferences
- The result depends heavily on when you bought:
  - A buyer at the Nov 2021 peak (just above $69,000) is up only about 7% after five years at $73,938, roughly 1.4% a year.
  - A buyer at the Oct 2025 record is down about 41%.
  - A buyer at the Jun 2026 low is up about 25%.
  - Ether at $2,055 is about 58% below its Aug 2025 record ($4,946) and below its 2021 high, so ether holders since 2021 are deeply under water.
  - (Calculated from the Fortune, Decrypt, CoinDesk and Bitcoin.com figures above.)
- The ~59%/yr ten-year figure for bitcoin comes mostly from its early years and from survivorship. Bitcoin is the survivor among millions of tokens, most of which went to zero. Its five-year return (about 13-14%/yr) was roughly equal to the S&P 500's, with about four times the volatility.
- The BIS "most users lost money" snapshot was taken in Dec 2022, near a cycle low; the FTX filing-date price was $16,871. At Nov 2026 prices (~4.4x higher), many 2015-2022 buyers who held on would now be in profit. However, the BIS also documented retail buying after big rallies and large holders selling to small ones. No study measures realized retail outcomes at 2026 prices.
- Realistic downside for planning: about -50% to -85% for bitcoin (-53% even in the "mild" 2026 bear), about -70% to -94% for ether, and close to -100% for most altcoins and memecoins. Fraud is a separate route to total loss, with an average reported crypto-linked loss of $62,604 in 2025. Reported IC3 losses likely understate the true total, because not all victims report.
- Spot ETFs (0.15-0.25%/yr) remove exchange-failure, withdrawal-freeze and self-custody risks, and they sit inside ordinary or retirement brokerage accounts. They do not reduce price risk. Holding coins directly adds platform risk (Celsius, FTX) and scam exposure.

### Gaps

- Exact ether 2026 low and Nov 2021 peak values were not confirmed by a primary price source.

## 2. Leveraged and inverse ETFs (including single-stock leveraged ETFs)

### Takeaway
Daily-reset leveraged and inverse ETFs deliver their stated multiple for one day only. Over weeks or months, volatility and financing costs can wipe out holders even when the underlying asset rises: a 2x MicroStrategy ETF fell 65% in a year when the stock rose 28%. Single-stock versions have done worst. Morningstar (data to Aug 2026) finds the median single-stock ETF down 38% since launch, 19% down more than 75%, and only 18% beating the stock they track. Regulators treat these as one-day trading tools: FINRA since 2009, and the SEC blocked plans for more than 2x leverage in Dec 2025.

### Cited Findings

#### How daily reset causes "volatility decay"
- WSJ worked example (Oct 2025): a stock starts at $10, falls 30% on Monday and gains 50% on Tuesday to end at $10.50 (+5%). A 2x ETF on it falls 60% to $4, then rises 100% to only $8 (-20%) — [WSJ via Tiger Brokers, 23 Oct 2025](https://www.itiger.com/news/2577374910)
- FINRA Regulatory Notice 09-31 (Jun 2009; older but still standing guidance): daily-reset leveraged and inverse ETFs "typically are unsuitable for retail investors who plan to hold them for longer than one trading session, particularly in volatile markets" — [FINRA](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/notices/09-31)
- Guedj, Li & McCann, "Leveraged ETFs, Holding Periods and Investment Shortfalls" (Journal of Index Investing, 2010; older):
  - Holding periods were inferred from trading volume.
  - A substantial share of investors hold these funds longer than one or two days, some longer than a quarter.
  - Holders can lose about 3% in under three weeks, roughly a 50% annualized cost.
  - Source: [SLCG paper](https://www.slcg.com/files/research-papers/Leveraged%20ETFs,%20Holding%20Periods%20and%20Investment%20Shortfalls.pdf)
- Tracking shortfalls (Morningstar, late 2025, via search excerpts): leveraged single-stock ETFs often miss even their one-day targets.
  - The shortfall cost traders about $3.3B in returns from launch through 24 Oct 2025, not counting about $230M in fees.
  - Morningstar blames swap financing costs, which grew as fund assets grew.
  - Source: [Morningstar](https://morningstar.com/funds/these-etfs-had-one-job-they-didnt-do-it-costing-traders-billions)
  - A secondary summary says traders received $10.1B instead of the $13.4B they would have earned had the funds hit their targets — [Firstlinks](https://www.firstlinks.com.au/leveraged-single-stock-etfs-dont-work-as-advertised)
- Hanyang Securities simulation (Korea, Jul 2026): single-stock leveraged ETFs on chip stocks could lose up to 75% over a year even if the underlying stocks end where they started — [Seoul Economic Daily, 30 Jul 2026](https://en.sedaily.com/finance/2026/07/30/single-stock-leverage-etfs-face-up-to-75-percent-loss-even)

#### Concrete outcomes and retail usage
- WSJ (23 Oct 2025):
  - The Defiance Daily Target 2x Long MSTR ETF was down 65% while Strategy (MSTR) shares rose 28% over the same 12 months.
  - Leveraged single-stock ETFs held about $40B by mid-Oct 2025 (VettaFi).
  - About 200 leveraged equity ETFs launched in 2025, bringing the total to 701 (Bank of America).
  - Fees are typically about 1% a year, vs. 0.3% on average for active funds.
  - Investors pulled almost $5B over two quarters, the category's first outflows (Morningstar).
  - A Reddit holder: "If I did not understand price decay before, I do now."
  - Source: [WSJ via Tiger Brokers](https://www.itiger.com/news/2577374910)
- Morningstar, "These 'Innovative' ETFs Have Been Disastrous for Investors" (Sept 2026; 518 single-stock ETFs; data as of 14 Aug 2026; figures from search excerpts because the page returned 403):
  - The median single-stock ETF is down 38% since inception.
  - 19% have lost more than 75%.
  - Only 18% outperformed the stock they track.
  - Investors paid more than $500M in management fees over the four years to July 2026.
  - Source: [Morningstar](https://www.morningstar.com/funds/these-innovative-etfs-have-been-disastrous-investors)
  - A companion piece attributes the 38% median loss specifically to leveraged single-stock ETFs — [Morningstar](https://www.morningstar.com/stocks/best-stock-picker-recent-years-marketing-department)
- ETF Action (30 Jul 2026):
  - Leveraged single-stock ETFs took in $35.4B of lifetime flows but held only $29.9B of assets, a gap of more than $5.4B of value destroyed. This is a rough flows-minus-assets estimate.
  - The simple average 12-month return was -26.8%.
  - The best fund, MUU (2x Micron), rose nearly 2,000% in 12 months; the worst, SMU, fell 99%.
  - Source: [ETF Action](https://www.etfaction.com/leverage-run-amok-the-single-stock-etf-booms-winners-wipeouts-and-5b-hole/)
- Demand is still rising: record net inflows of $30.8B into leveraged and inverse single-stock ETFs in Jan-Jul 2026, above the $22.6B for all of 2025 (Morningstar data) — [Paperjam](https://en.paperjam.lu/article/leveraged-single-stock-etfs-bad-in-up-markets-worst-in-down-markets)
- South Korea, 2026 (non-US, but the clearest measured retail outcome):
  - Retail investors lost about 6.3 trillion won on newly launched single-stock leveraged and inverse products between the 27 May launch and 14 Aug 2026.
  - That was 2.3242 trillion won in realized trading losses plus 3.9888 trillion won in losses on positions still held.
  - 62% of the losses fell on investors in their 40s and 50s.
  - Sources: [Asia Business Daily, 21 Oct 2026](https://www.asiae.co.kr/en/article/2026102113361689828); [Herald Business](https://biz.heraldcorp.com/article/10910365)
  - The finance minister apologized — [CNBC, 29 Jul 2026](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/29/korea-leveraged-etf-kodex-sk-hynix.html)
  - Regulators may cut the leverage multiple — [Korea Times, 22 Oct 2026](https://www.koreatimes.co.kr/amp/economy/others/20261022/single-stock-leveraged-etfs-reignite-turf-war-between-financial-regulators)
  - A Citi estimate of $38.7B in retail leveraged-ETF losses also circulates, but its scope is unclear — [Korea JoongAng Daily](https://www.koreajoongangdaily.com/business/citi-estimates-retail-investors-have-lost-387-billion-on-leveraged-etfs/12798964)

#### Regulation and how money comes out
- SEC, early Dec 2025: nine letters to sponsors including Direxion, ProShares and Tidal. The SEC said it would not review proposed ETFs with more than 2x leverage, citing the rule that caps fund leverage risk at 200% of an unleveraged version of the same holdings.
  - ProShares moved to withdraw some 3x filings; Volatility Shares had filed for 5x funds.
  - No 3x single-stock ETF exists in the US.
  - Sources: [WealthManagement/Bloomberg, 3 Dec 2025](https://www.wealthmanagement.com/etfs/sec-halts-high-leveraged-etf-plans-in-warning-over-risks); [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-12-03/sec-halts-high-leveraged-etf-plans-in-warning-over-risk-control)
- Exit and closure risk: Volatility Shares' proposed 5x funds would have been liquidated if the underlying stock fell 20% in one day. David Einhorn shorted the leveraged MicroStrategy ETFs, saying "These products are destined to fail" — [WSJ via Tiger Brokers](https://www.itiger.com/news/2577374910)

### Inferences
- Rule of thumb for decay (my calculation, standard continuous-time approximation, before fees and financing):
  - A daily-reset 2x fund lags twice the index's compound return by about σ² a year (σ = annual volatility); a 3x fund lags three times the index by about 3σ².
  - For an index with 20% volatility: about 4%/yr drag at 2x and about 12%/yr at 3x.
  - For a single stock with 60% volatility at 2x: about 36%/yr drag. Add about 1% fees plus swap financing.
  - This explains outcomes like the MSTR 2x fund (-65% while the stock rose 28%) and the Korean simulation (-75% with a flat underlying).
- Two-day illustration: +10% then -10%. The index ends -1%, a 2x fund -4%, a 3x fund -9%, and a -2x inverse fund -4%. Inverse funds decay too.
- Upside exists only when the underlying trends strongly with little chop (MUU +~2,000%). The distribution is extremely skewed: the median fund lost 38%, and most underperformed even the plain stock. For a buy-and-hold retail investor, the realistic expectation is underperformance, with a high chance of 75-99% losses on single-stock versions.
- How money comes out: like any ETF, these trade during market hours. Products can be closed or liquidated, and gaps in volatile markets can crystallize losses.

### Gaps
- No US study was found measuring the fraction of retail holders who profited from leveraged or inverse ETFs (Korea's regulator data is the closest).
- Classic long-run examples (e.g., TQQQ vs. QQQ in 2022, SQQQ since inception) were not verified this session.
- The SEC/FINRA investor alert on investor.gov returned 403, so its numerical example was not quoted.
- The Morningstar 2025-26 figures come from search excerpts, not the full text.

## 3. Individual stock picking

### Takeaway
Most individual US stocks have done worse than one-month Treasury bills over their lifetimes; only 41% beat T-bills in Bessembinder's 1926-2025 update. Just 46 stocks account for half of the $91 trillion in shareholder wealth created. About 40% of stocks suffer permanent 70%+ declines, and catastrophic losers often look healthy at their peak. Individual investors who trade actively trail the market (Barber & Odean). Retail stock-picking frenzies such as GameStop in 2021 and Opendoor in 2025 produced a few big winners while most participants lost.

### Cited Findings
- Bessembinder, "Do Stocks Outperform Treasury Bills?" (Journal of Financial Economics 2018; data 1926-2016; older):
  - About 58% of stocks failed to beat T-bills over their lifetimes.
  - The best-performing ~4% of companies explain the stock market's entire net gain since 1926; the other 96% collectively matched T-bills.
  - Sources: [ASU W. P. Carey](https://wpcarey.asu.edu/department-finance/faculty-research/do-stocks-outperform-treasury-bills); [ASU news](https://news.wpcarey.asu.edu/20180917-do-stocks-outperform-treasury-bills); [JFE](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2018.06.004)
  - ASU says 86 stocks accounted for half of the $16T total (through 2016); Morningstar says 89 in the 2017 study (minor discrepancy).
- Bessembinder's latest update, "One Hundred Years in the U.S. Stock Markets" (1926-2025; about 30,000 stocks):
  - Only 41.17% outperformed T-bills.
  - "less than half posted positive returns."
  - 46 stocks accounted for half of the $91 trillion in wealth created.
  - Top creators named include Nvidia, Alphabet, Tesla, Coca-Cola, GM and J&J.
  - Source: [Morningstar, 17 Jun 2026](https://indexes.morningstar.com/insights/perspective/blt1a0584197b381618/why-most-stocks-arent-worth-owning)
- Bessembinder global study (1991-2020; more than 64,000 stocks; older): 55.2% of US stocks and 57.4% of non-US stocks underperformed T-bills. The top 2.4% of firms account for all $75.7T of net global wealth creation — [ASU W. P. Carey](https://wpcarey.asu.edu/department-finance/faculty-research/do-stocks-outperform-treasury-bills)
- J.P. Morgan, "The Agony & the Ecstasy" (2014; Russell 3000, 1980-2014, about 13,000 stocks; older):
  - "roughly 40% of all stocks have suffered a permanent 70%+ decline from their peak value."
  - The median stock returned -54% relative to the Russell 3000 index.
  - Two-thirds of all stocks underperformed the index, and 40% had negative absolute returns.
  - Source: [J.P. Morgan 2014 PDF](https://www.chase.com/content/dam/privatebanking/en/mobile/documents/eotm/eotm_2014_09_02_agonyescstasy.pdf)
- J.P. Morgan "Agony & Ecstasy Part IV" (2024) defines a catastrophic decline as a 70% fall from peak without recovery. At their peaks, catastrophic decliners looked healthy:
  - 54% were profitable, and 63% had net debt of 2x EBITDA or less.
  - Most traded at "reasonable" forward P/E multiples.
  - Wall Street consensus was "highly tilted to 'strong buy'," with about half projected to rise further.
  - Source: [J.P. Morgan 2024 PDF](https://www.jpmorgan.com/content/dam/jpmorgan/documents/wealth-management/the-agony-and-the-ecstasy-2024.pdf)
- Barber & Odean, "Trading Is Hazardous to Your Wealth" (Journal of Finance 2000; 66,465 households at a discount broker, 1991-96; older):
  - The households that traded most earned 11.4% a year, while the market returned 17.9%.
  - The average household earned 16.4% and turned over 75% of its portfolio a year.
  - Source: [Journal of Finance](https://doi.org/10.1111/0022-1082.00226)
- Barber, Huang, Odean & Schwarz, "Attention-Induced Trading and Returns: Evidence from Robinhood Users" (Journal of Finance 2022; older):
  - Herding by Robinhood users predicts negative returns; the top stocks bought each day averaged -4.7% abnormal returns over the next 20 days (paper abstract) — [Journal of Finance](https://doi.org/10.1111/jofi.13183)
  - Odean: "While some Robinhood users undoubtedly made money… a greater number of them lost money" — [Newswise Q&A](https://www.newswise.com/articles/how-robinhood-s-trading-app-spurs-investors-herding-instincts-qa-with-prof-odean)
- 2021 meme stocks (older):
  - Wealthsimple's own data: 67% of its users who traded GameStop lost money, and 86% of them lost under $1,000 — [Globe and Mail](https://arc-dev.theglobeandmail.com/investing/markets/inside-the-market/article-young-men-losing-money-wealthsimple-study-profiles-gamestop-investors)
  - Brokerage-account evidence (Hasso, Müller, Pelster & Warkulat, Finance Research Letters 2022) concludes the GameStop frenzy was speculative trading rather than a protest, and many retail investors also shorted GME — [RePEc/IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finlet/v45y2022ics154461232100221x.html)
  - In a 2021 poll, 28% of Americans said they bought GameStop or other viral stocks in Jan 2021 — [Yahoo Finance-Harris Poll](https://www.yahoo.com/now/gamestop-amc-reddit-investing-213609595.html)
- 2025-26 meme episode, Opendoor (OPEN):
  - Rose about 264% in 2025 from a record low of $0.51 in June, driven by Reddit/X-fueled retail buying — [Motley Fool, 27 Jan 2026](https://www.fool.com/investing/2026/01/27/this-stock-soared-by-264-in-2025-heres-happen-2026/)
  - Then declined steadily after Sept 2025 leadership changes; reports in early 2026 put it about half below its peak (secondary source) — [IndexBox](https://www.indexbox.io/blog/opendoor-stock-declines-after-2025-meme-stock-rally/)
- Concentration guidance: Jason Zweig's rule, "Never put more than 10% of your net worth into any one investment" — [Ritholtz, 2015](https://ritholtz.com/2015/09/jason-zweigs-rules-for-investing/)

### Inferences
- Odds for a single stock: across the century, a randomly picked US stock was more likely than not to trail T-bills (41% beat them) and very likely to trail the index (about two-thirds underperformed, per J.P. Morgan 1980-2014).
- The upside is real but rare: 46 of about 30,000 stocks produced half of all wealth. A concentrated stock picker is betting on owning one of those few big winners.
- Profitable, "reasonably valued," analyst-favored companies still suffered permanent 70%+ crashes. Doing your homework does not protect you from a disaster in any one stock.
- The 6.5-percentage-point annual shortfall for the most active traders (11.4% vs. 17.9%) compounds dramatically. Over 20 years at those rates, $10,000 grows to about $87,000 vs. about $269,000 (my calculation).
- Meme-stock frenzies follow a pattern: a late-arriving majority loses, often modest sums, while a minority profits. Measured shares of losing traders run about 60-70% (Wealthsimple GME).

### Gaps
- No hard aggregate data was found on retail losses from the 2021 meme stocks into the 2022 bear market, such as broker-wide performance or Vanda Research portfolio estimates. GME/AMC peak-to-trough figures were not verified.
- No reliable aggregate measure was found for 2024-2026 retail stock-picking episodes (2024 GameStop revival, 2025-26 Opendoor/"meme" surges). Coverage is mostly single-stock news, and the Opendoor drawdown figures are secondary.
- A figure of 27.6% of stocks beating the value-weighted market in the 1926-2025 Bessembinder update appeared only in unverified secondary coverage.
- The 2024 J.P. Morgan update did not restate an overall share of stocks with catastrophic declines in the text checked.

## 4. Realistic upside, realistic downside, and the fraction of retail participants who end up ahead (by category)

### Takeaway
In every category the payoff is lopsided: a small minority captures very large gains while the typical participant does worse than a plain index fund, and often loses money. Where outcomes have been measured, most participants lost or underperformed.

### Cited Findings
- **Bitcoin (holding):**
  - Upside: about 59%/yr over 10 years per one tracker, and about 14%/yr over 5 years — [PortfoliosLab](https://portfolioslab.com/symbol/BTC-USD); [MoneyLion](https://moneylion.com/trending/money/1k-in-bitcoin-sp-500-gold-which-investment-10-years-ago-won)
  - Downside: 77-85% bear markets historically, and -53% at the Jun 2026 low — [CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2026/10/06/from-retail-leverage-to-etf-flows-a-year-after-its-record-high-bitcoin-is-down-just-32)
  - Share ahead: a BIS estimate found a majority of app users (roughly 73-81%; over four-fifths under monthly buying) had lost money as of Dec 2022 (older) — [CoinDesk/Yahoo](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/retail-crypto-investors-emerging-economies-162932915.html); [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2022-11-16/vast-majority-of-retail-investors-in-bitcoin-lost-money-bis-says)
- **Ether:**
  - Downside: -93.8% (2018), about -80% (2022), and about -67% to -70% (2026 low vs. Aug 2025 record) — [CoinMarketCap Academy](https://coinmarketcap.com/academy/article/a-comparison-of-the-2018-bear-market-and-2022-crypto-market-drawdown); [Bitcoin.com News](https://news.bitcoin.com/ethereum-sits-at-1644-10-months-after-its-4946-all-time-high-what-changed/)
  - Current: $2,054.82 on 11 Nov 2026 — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/article/price-of-bitcoin-11-11-2026/)
- **Altcoins and memecoins:**
  - Downside: 53.2% of tokens tracked since 2021 are dead, and 98.6% of pump.fun tokens showed rug-pull or pump-and-dump traits — [CoinGecko](https://www.coingecko.com/research/publications/how-many-cryptocurrencies-failed); [Solidus Labs](https://www.soliduslabs.com/reports/solana-rug-pulls-pump-dumps-crypto-compliance)
  - Share ahead: most active pump.fun traders lost money each month from Apr 2024 to late 2025, and only 5.37% made more than $1,000 even in the strong month of Apr 2026 — [CoinGecko](https://www.coingecko.com/research/publications/pump-fun-traders-are-making-a-comeback)
  - About 0.41% realized more than $10,000 — [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/300403/pump-fun-traders-millionaires); fewer than 0.01% made over $1M — [crypto.news](https://crypto.news/over-60-pump-fun-traders-saw-losses-less-than-0-01-made-over-1m/)
- **Crypto scams (the most common route to a total loss):** $11.366B in crypto-linked losses in 2025, averaging $62,604 per complaint — [FBI IC3](https://www.ic3.gov/AnnualReport/Reports/2025_IC3Report.pdf)
- **Single-stock leveraged ETFs:**
  - Median -38% since inception; 19% lost more than 75%; only 18% beat their underlying stock (to Aug 2026) — [Morningstar](https://www.morningstar.com/funds/these-innovative-etfs-have-been-disastrous-investors)
  - Best case +~2,000% (MUU); worst -99% (SMU) — [ETF Action](https://www.etfaction.com/leverage-run-amok-the-single-stock-etf-booms-winners-wipeouts-and-5b-hole/)
  - Korean retail lost 6.3 trillion won in about 2.5 months — [Asia Business Daily](https://www.asiae.co.kr/en/article/2026102113361689828)
- **Individual stocks:**
  - Only 41% beat T-bills over their lifetimes (1926-2025) — [Morningstar](https://indexes.morningstar.com/insights/perspective/blt1a0584197b381618/why-most-stocks-arent-worth-owning)
  - About one-third beat the Russell 3000, and about 40% suffered permanent 70%+ declines (1980-2014, older) — [J.P. Morgan](https://www.chase.com/content/dam/privatebanking/en/mobile/documents/eotm/eotm_2014_09_02_agonyescstasy.pdf)
- **Active retail stock traders:** the most active households trailed the market by 6.5 points a year, and the average household by 1.5 points (1991-96, older) — [Barber & Odean](https://doi.org/10.1111/0022-1082.00226)
- **Meme-stock traders:** 67% of Wealthsimple GME traders lost money (2021, older) — [Globe and Mail](https://arc-dev.theglobeandmail.com/investing/markets/inside-the-market/article-young-men-losing-money-wealthsimple-study-profiles-gamestop-investors)

### Inferences
- Early bitcoin holders who held through repeated 75-85% crashes made life-changing gains, but that required both early entry and unusual patience. Measured retail behavior (buying after rallies, selling in crashes, retail buying while whales sold) means the typical user's experience was far worse than bitcoin's headline return.
- For a willing risk-taker, sensible planning assumptions are:
  - **Bitcoin:** plausibly positive long-run expected return, but with a 50-85% drop possible in any cycle.
  - **Ether and other large altcoins:** deeper drawdowns.
  - **Memecoins and new tokens:** treat as lottery tickets with a likely total loss.
  - **Leveraged ETFs held longer than days:** expect underperformance and possible wipeout.
  - **A handful of individual stocks:** more likely than not to trail an index fund, with a meaningful chance of a permanent 70%+ loss in any one name.
- How easy it is to get money out, from easiest to hardest:
  - Exchange-listed products (spot crypto ETFs, leveraged ETFs, stocks) can be sold on any trading day, but leveraged funds can close or reset, and gaps in volatile markets can lock in losses.
  - Coins held on platforms can be frozen (Celsius) or repaid at bankruptcy-date prices (FTX).
  - Scam "platforms" never return money.

### Gaps
- No single study measures the share of US retail participants who are ahead across all three categories. The fractions above come from different periods, countries and methods and should not be compared directly.

## 5. Reputable guidance on limiting speculation (e.g., capping speculative money at a small fixed share)

### Takeaway
Mainstream guidance converges on keeping speculation small and walled off. Wealth managers cap crypto at roughly 0-5% of a portfolio, depending on risk tolerance; Morgan Stanley tops out at 4%, and BlackRock calls 1-2% bitcoin "reasonable." The "mad money" approach (Benjamin Graham, popularized by Jason Zweig) keeps a separate account of about 5-10% that is never topped up after losses. Regulators treat leveraged ETFs as intraday or one-day tools, not holdings.

### Cited Findings
- Morgan Stanley Global Investment Committee (note dated 1 Oct 2025):
  - Crypto caps by portfolio type: up to 4% for "opportunistic growth," about 3% for "market growth," about 2% for "balanced growth," and 0% for wealth-conservation and income portfolios.
  - Rebalance regularly because of the potential for "greater drawdowns and outsized volatility."
  - Source: [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/373562/morgan-stanley-sets-4-crypto-cap-opportunistic-portfolios-aligning-blackrock-grayscale)
- Other firms (same source):
  - BlackRock has called a 1%-2% bitcoin weighting a "reasonable range."
  - Fidelity research suggests 2%-5% under bullish adoption scenarios.
  - Grayscale, a crypto issuer with a commercial interest, suggests about 5%.
  - Source: [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/373562/morgan-stanley-sets-4-crypto-cap-opportunistic-portfolios-aligning-blackrock-grayscale)
- Vanguard publishes no allocation. It now allows third-party crypto ETFs (from 2 Dec 2025) but excludes memecoin funds and launches no products of its own — [ThinkAdvisor](https://www.thinkadvisor.com/2025/12/01/vanguard-will-now-allow-crypto-etfs-on-its-platform/)
- "Mad money" (an idea traced to Benjamin Graham): Morningstar suggests limiting mad-money investments to 5% or 10% of a portfolio — [Morningstar UK, "What Is Mad Money?"](https://morningstar.co.uk/uk/news/211303/what-is-mad-money.aspx); [Morningstar UK, "Speculate Without Losing Your Shirt"](https://morningstar.co.uk/uk/news/63171/speculate-without-losing-your-shirt.aspx)
- Jason Zweig (WealthTrack): set up a separate account for speculating with a small slice of the portfolio. Never add more money to it, and if you lose it all, close it down — [WealthTrack](https://wealthtrack.com/timeless-wisdom/)
- Zweig's rule on single positions: "Never put more than 10% of your net worth into any one investment" — [Ritholtz, 2015](https://ritholtz.com/2015/09/jason-zweigs-rules-for-investing/)
- Leveraged ETFs: FINRA (2009) says they are "typically… unsuitable" for retail investors holding longer than one session — [FINRA RN 09-31](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/notices/09-31). The SEC (Dec 2025) refused to review funds above 2x — [WealthManagement/Bloomberg](https://www.wealthmanagement.com/etfs/sec-halts-high-leveraged-etf-plans-in-warning-over-risks)

### Inferences
- A defensible, evidence-consistent rule for a non-expert who wants to take risk:
  - Keep all speculation (crypto, leveraged ETFs, individual stock bets) at about 5% of investable assets, 10% at most, in a separate account.
  - Within that slice, keep crypto at or below the 1-4% institutional ranges.
  - Size it so a 75-100% loss would not change your life.
  - Never add money after losses.
  - Rebalance or skim winnings back to the core portfolio.
  - Prefer spot ETFs over coins held on platforms.
  - Treat leveraged ETFs as day-trading tools only.
- These caps come from practitioners. They are not derived from academic optimization, and the bank and issuer numbers come from firms that sell crypto products.

### Gaps
- Neither the SEC nor FINRA publishes a numeric cap for speculative allocations.
- I found no primary source in which Zweig himself sets a 10% cap specifically on a "mad money" account. The 10% figure circulates mainly in secondhand quotes, while Morningstar's guidance is "5% or 10%."
- Vanguard's own speculation-limit guidance (if any) was not found.
