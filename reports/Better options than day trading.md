# Earn more, trade less, compound the rest

The better opportunities are not a smarter way to trade but a sequence ranked by certainty: collect the guaranteed returns first, let a low-cost index fund compound in accounts you can still reach, and point your appetite for risk at your own earning power, where a Luau developer has a real edge, instead of at markets, where the typical retail player measurably loses. A **401(k) match pays an instant 50% to 100%** on matched dollars, yet about 42% of workers miss part of it. Paying off a credit card retires debt charging **22.36%** on average, and safe cash in T-bills, money market funds or top savings accounts pays about **3.75% to 4.5%** (I bonds **5.51%**), roughly the 3.7% inflation rate. The account you describe, one that grows while letting you pull money out, is best approximated by a **Roth IRA holding a total-market or S&P 500 index fund**: up to $7,500 of 2026 contributions can come back out at any time without tax or penalty, and US stocks have returned about **10% a year since 1928**, interrupted by crashes of 34% to 57%. Every higher-risk bet on your list, from crypto and leveraged ETFs to stock picking, 0DTE options, prediction markets and sports betting, has either lost money for most measured participants or trailed a plain index fund, while the winnings collect with a small minority, the house or the market makers. The largest lever at your stage is income: an extra $500 a month equals a 7% return on about $86,000, and freelance scripting at roughly **$25 to $60 an hour** for developers with a live game is far steadier than a Roblox game of your own, where the **median DevEx participant collected about $1,500** in a year and most monetizing creators never cash out at all. "A lot" is still within reach, since $1,000 a month invested at 7% grows to about **$1.17 million in 30 years**, but it arrives by compounding earned and saved dollars, not through a hot streak. Anyone under 18 follows the same logic through an adult custodian, needs earned income for a custodial Roth IRA, and cannot cash out Roblox earnings before age 13.

## Only the boring options pass all five tests

The scorecard rates each option on the five tests in the question: what a typical participant can expect to earn, how bad a realistic bad outcome gets, how quickly money comes back out, how much time it takes, and how ordinary people have actually done. "Expected return" means what a typical participant should plan on, not what the winners made, because in the speculative and product categories a few enormous outcomes inflate the averages. The last column deserves the most weight. With guaranteed instruments everyone earns the posted rate, so regular people do exactly as well as professionals; with broad index funds, investors kept nearly all of the return; and in every speculative category where outcomes have been measured, most ordinary participants lost money or lagged. Three tiers emerge. The eight core rows need little time and deliver positive results to nearly everyone who uses them, and their main risks, a stock crash or inflation outrunning cash, are survivable with patience. The earning rows also have positive medians, but they cost hours, and their averages overstate what a typical person makes. The seven speculative rows are the only ones where the typical participant has measurably ended up behind, even though each has famous winners, and several of them, 0DTE options above all, demand attention during the market hours a day job already claims.

## Guaranteed returns and safe cash come before any market bet

### A 401(k) match pays 50 to 100 cents per dollar on day one

The most common employer formula in Vanguard's 2026 study is **50 cents per dollar on the first 6% of pay**; the average promised match is 4.7% of pay (median 4.0%), and collecting all of it takes a deferral of about 6.4% on average ([Vanguard How America Saves 2026](https://workplace.vanguard.com/content/dam/inst/iig-transformation/has/2026/pdf/HowAmericaSaves2026.pdf)). For someone earning $50,000, contributing 6%, or $3,000, under that formula brings in $1,500 of employer money, a 50% return before the market moves, and plans that match 100% of the first 4% or 5% double the money outright. Nothing else in this report reliably comes close. Yet in Vanguard's analysis of 2023 plan data, 16% of workers contributed nothing and another 26% contributed below the match cap, so **about 42% did not collect the full match**; only 22% of workers earning under $30,000 captured all of it, and roughly 20% of those earning $150,000 or more still left some behind ([Vanguard](https://corporate.vanguard.com/content/dam/corp/research/pdf/better_match_formulas_401k_plans.pdf)).

The price of the match is reduced access. A 401(k) loan is generally capped at the lesser of $50,000 or half the vested balance, must be repaid within five years, and turns into a taxable distribution if it defaults ([IRS](https://www.irs.gov/retirement-plans/retirement-plans-faqs-regarding-loans)). Early withdrawals are taxable, and the exceptions to the 10% penalty are narrow, such as one emergency withdrawal of up to $1,000 a year or $5,000 per birth or adoption ([IRS](https://www.irs.gov/retirement-plans/plan-participant-employee/retirement-topics-exceptions-to-tax-on-early-distributions)). People do tap these accounts: 13% of participants had a loan outstanding in 2025, and 6% took a hardship withdrawal, up from 2% in 2021 ([Vanguard](https://workplace.vanguard.com/content/dam/inst/iig-transformation/has/2026/pdf/HowAmericaSaves2026.pdf)). Vesting schedules, which can claw back unvested match from people who leave early, were outside this research, so read your plan's rule before counting on the money. The 2026 employee limit is **$24,500** ([IRS](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/401k-limit-increases-to-24500-for-2026-ira-limit-increases-to-7500)); Milliman projects $25,500 for 2027, a figure the IRS had not confirmed as of November 11 ([Milliman](https://www.milliman.com/en/insight/2027-irs-limits-forecast-final-estimates)).

### Paying off a 22% card beats any market investment

The Federal Reserve's latest G.19 release puts the average rate on credit card accounts that are charged interest at **22.36% in the third quarter of 2026** ([Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/g19/current/default.htm)). Every dollar of balance paid off earns that rate with certainty and without tax, more than double the stock market's long-run return. A $5,000 balance costs about $1,118 a year in interest; the same $5,000 in an index fund would be expected to earn $375 to $500, with a real chance of a loss. Cheaper debt is a closer call. The same release shows 60-month new-car loans at 7.54%, roughly level with expected stock returns, and 24-month personal loans at 11.90%, still worth paying down first. The order that follows from the arithmetic is a small cash cushion, then the full match (a 50–100% instant return beats 22%), then high-rate debt, then investing.

### Safe cash pays about 4%, which only keeps pace with inflation

The backdrop shifted this fall. Consumer prices rose **3.7% in the year to September 2026**, driven by energy, and that is the latest 12-month reading because the 2025 funding lapse left no October 2025 baseline for the newest release ([BLS](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/cpi_10142026.htm); [BLS](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/cpi.nr0.htm)). The Fed **raised** its target range to 3.75–4.00% on September 16 and held there on October 28, with two officials voting for another hike ([Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20260916a.htm); [Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20261028a.htm)). Against that, the best high-yield savings accounts pay about 4.0–4.35% on standard terms, with conditional promotional offers near 5%, while the national average savings rate is just 0.38–0.57% ([Bankrate](https://www.bankrate.com/banking/savings/rates/); [NerdWallet](https://www.nerdwallet.com/banking/best-savings-accounts); [FDIC](https://www.fdic.gov/national-rates-and-rate-caps)). The Crane 100 index of large money market funds yielded 3.75% on November 10 ([Crane Data](https://cranedata.com/)). On the same date, Treasury bills paid **3.94% for four weeks, 4.22% for three months and 4.54% for one year**, from a $100 minimum and free of state and local tax ([US Treasury](https://home.treasury.gov/resource-center/data-chart-center/interest-rates/TextView?type=daily_treasury_bill_rates&field_tdr_date_value_month=202611); [TreasuryDirect](https://www.treasurydirect.gov/marketable-securities/treasury-bills/)). I bonds bought from November 2026 through April 2027 pay **5.51%**, including a **1.40% fixed rate**, the highest since 2006, that lasts for the life of the bond; the catch is a $10,000 annual cap per person, a 12-month lockup, and the loss of three months' interest if cashed within five years ([TreasuryDirect](https://www.treasurydirect.gov/savings-bonds/i-bonds/); [Tipswatch](https://tipswatch.com/2026/10/30/treasury-sets-i-bonds-fixed-rate-of-1-40-composite-rate-rises-to-5-51/)).

These rates protect money; they do not grow it. A 4.3% savings yield taxed at 22% leaves about 3.35%, slightly below 3.7% inflation, so cash is the right home for an emergency fund and for anything you need within a few years, not for building wealth. Within that role the choices sort cleanly: a high-yield savings account or money fund for money you might need this week, T-bills for a few more tenths of a point and no state tax, and I bonds for safe money you will not touch for at least a year. Savings accounts and money funds float with the Fed while a T-bill or I bond locks its rate for its term, a distinction that matters while the Fed is still leaning toward hikes.

## A Roth IRA of index funds is the put-in, take-out account you want

### Stocks have paid about 10% a year, with crashes you have to sit through

The S&P 500, dividends included, compounded at about **10.0% a year from 1928 through 2025** and about 14.7% a year over 2016–2025 ([Damodaran, NYU Stern](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/New_Home_Page/datafile/histretSP.html)). Over 1900–2025, US stocks returned 9.8% a year nominal and **6.6% after inflation**, against 0.5% real for T-bills ([UBS Global Investment Returns Yearbook 2026](https://www.ubs.com/content/dam/assets/wm/static/cio/documents/giry2026-summary-public.pdf)). Forecasters expect less ahead: J.P. Morgan's October 2026 assumptions put US large caps at **7.5% a year** over the next 10 to 15 years and flag elevated valuations and concentration ([J.P. Morgan](https://am.jpmorgan.com/us/en/asset-management/institutional/about-us/media/press-releases/jp-morgan-releases-2027-long-term-capital-market-assumptions/)). Planning on 6–8% is more prudent than extrapolating the last decade.

The price of that return is drawdowns. Since 2000 the S&P 500 has fallen about 49% in the dot-com bust and needed roughly seven years to regain its peak; 56.8% in the financial crisis, regained in about five and a half years; 33.9% in 33 days in 2020, regained in six months; 25.4% in 2022, regained in two years; and 18.9% in the 2025 tariff shock, regained in four months. After 1929, the Dow took about 25 years to recover ([Newsweek](https://www.newsweek.com/midterm-stock-market-rout-compared-previous-crashes-12677266); [Shibui Finance](https://shibui.finance/sp500-drawdown-history)). The risk is not theoretical this month: on November 5, 2026, after the midterms, the S&P 500 fell **4.36% in a day** and the Nasdaq 5.52%, leaving the Nasdaq more than 10% below its October record ([Newsweek](https://www.newsweek.com/midterm-stock-market-rout-compared-previous-crashes-12677266)). Index fund money is liquid, because trades have settled on the next business day since May 2024 ([Davis Polk](https://www.davispolk.com/insights/client-update/sec-adopts-t-1-settlement-effective-may-2024)), but its value is not stable, so money needed within one to three years belongs in the cash layer.

The case for indexing rather than picking or trading rests on two measurements. Summaries of S&P's mid-2026 SPIVA scorecard show **90.49% of actively managed large-cap funds trailed the S&P 500 over 15 years** and 92.61% over 20, and only 36.52% of large-cap funds survived 20 years at all ([Retirement Literacy Foundation](https://retirementliteracyfoundation.org/guides/active-vs-index-funds-spiva/)). Morningstar's 2026 *Mind the Gap* study found the average dollar in US funds earned 8.7% a year over the decade to 2025 against 9.9% for the funds themselves, a 1.2-point annual penalty for buying and selling at the wrong times. Investors in US stock index funds lost almost nothing to timing (13.7% versus 13.8%), while holders of the most volatile funds gave up 2.1 points a year and crypto ETF holders reportedly about 14 ([Morningstar](https://www.morningstar.com/content/cs-assets/v3/assets/blt9415ea4cc4157833/blt852a4301adc2f5db/6a7373d51f7b5ae2da78e1e8/US_Mind_the_Gap_2026.pdf); [WealthManagement.com](https://www.wealthmanagement.com/investing-strategies/investors-miss-out-on-12-of-fund-returns-over-decade)). Morningstar's prescription reads like a direct answer to news-driven trading: hold the line on transactions, above all ad-hoc trades made in reaction to news, buy on a fixed schedule, and automate. Costs are now trivial, with VOO and VTI charging 0.03% a year, or $3 per $10,000 ([WTOP](https://wtop.com/news/2026/06/10-best-low-cost-index-funds-to-buy-in-2026-3/)), against 0.40% for the average equity mutual fund dollar ([ICI](https://www.ici.org/news-release/mutual-fund-and-etf-fees-remained-near-historic-lows-in-2025)).

### Roth rules let contributions come back out at any time

A Roth IRA turns that index fund into the flexible account you describe. For 2026 you can contribute up to **$7,500**, with full contributions allowed below $153,000 of income for single filers and $242,000 for married couples filing jointly ([IRS](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/401k-limit-increases-to-24500-for-2026-ira-limit-increases-to-7500)), and you need earned income at least equal to the contribution ([NerdWallet](https://www.nerdwallet.com/retirement/learn/why-your-kid-needs-a-roth-ira)). Withdrawals come out in a fixed order, regular contributions first, and getting your own contributions back is neither taxed nor penalized; only earnings must wait for age 59½ plus a five-year holding period, with exceptions such as a $10,000 first-home withdrawal ([IRS Publication 590-B](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p590b)). The catch is that contribution room spent on a withdrawal cannot be refilled beyond each year's limit, so the Roth works best as a backstop rather than a checking account. Put together, the account you are looking for is really four layers: cash in savings, a money fund or T-bills for emergencies and near-term spending; I bonds for safe money you can leave alone for a year; a Roth IRA as the reachable long-term core; and a taxable brokerage index fund for anything beyond the Roth limit, with the 401(k) collecting the match. Projections of the 2027 IRA limit conflict between $7,500 and $8,000, pending the IRS announcement expected this month ([UStax.tools](https://ustax.tools/retirement-contribution-limits-2027/); [The Finance Buff](https://thefinancebuff.com/401k-403b-ira-contribution-limits.html)).

### Taxes reward patience and punish fast trading

The holding period sets the tax rate. Gains on assets held more than a year are long-term, while net short-term gains are taxed as ordinary income ([IRS Topic 409](https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc409)). For 2026, the long-term rate is **0% up to $49,450 of taxable income for single filers** ($98,900 for joint filers) and 15% above that ([Kiplinger](https://www.kiplinger.com/taxes/irs-updates-capital-gains-tax-thresholds)); with the $16,100 standard deduction, a single person with about $65,550 of gross income can realize long-term gains at a zero federal rate ([IRS](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/irs-releases-tax-inflation-adjustments-for-tax-year-2026-including-amendments-from-the-one-big-beautiful-bill)). Every profitable day trade, by contrast, is short-term and lands in the ordinary brackets, where, per the same IRS release, 22% starts at $50,400 of taxable income and 24% at $105,700, plus state tax. Losses help less than traders hope. Net capital losses offset only $3,000 of ordinary income a year ([IRS Topic 409](https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc409)), and the wash-sale rule disallows a loss when you buy the same or a substantially identical security within 30 days before or after the sale, including purchases in your own IRA ([Kiplinger](https://www.kiplinger.com/taxes/604947/wash-sale-rule)). A frequent trader trips that rule constantly; a buy-and-hold indexer almost never does.

## Every high-risk bet on the list fails the typical player

The speculative options split into two families. Options, event contracts and sports bets are zero-sum or worse before costs, so every winner needs a loser and the platform takes a toll on each trade. Crypto tokens, single stocks and leveraged funds can rise in aggregate, but their payoffs are so lopsided that a few winners hide a losing majority. Trading on news and social media is the behavior the research punishes most consistently. The stocks most heavily bought each day by herding Robinhood users went on to underperform by an average 4.7% over the following 20 days ([Barber, Huang, Odean & Schwarz, *Journal of Finance*](https://doi.org/10.1111/jofi.13183)), and retail investors who piled into options ahead of earnings announcements lost 5–9% on average, and 10–14% around the announcements expected to be most volatile ([Stanford GSB](https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/faculty-research/publications/losing-optional-retail-option-trading-expected-announcement); [MIT Sloan](https://mitsloan.mit.edu/ideas-made-to-matter/retail-investors-lose-big-options-markets-research-shows)).

### Crypto pairs real upside with 50–85% crashes and rampant fraud

Bitcoin traded at **$73,938 on November 11, 2026**, down 29% in a year and about 41% below its October 2025 record of $126,198; ether, at $2,055, sat about 58% below its August 2025 peak ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/article/price-of-bitcoin-11-11-2026/); [Bitcoin.com News](https://news.bitcoin.com/ethereum-sits-at-1644-10-months-after-its-4946-all-time-high-what-changed/)). This cycle's low came on June 30, just under $59,000, more than 53% off the top, and earlier bitcoin bear markets cut 77–85% ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2026/10/06/from-retail-leverage-to-etf-flows-a-year-after-its-record-high-bitcoin-is-down-just-32)); ether fell 93.8% in 2018 ([CoinMarketCap Academy](https://coinmarketcap.com/academy/article/a-comparison-of-the-2018-bear-market-and-2022-crypto-market-drawdown)). The headline long-run gains are real but depend heavily on timing. Over the five years to July 2026, bitcoin gained about 88% against 87% for the S&P 500 ([MoneyLion](https://moneylion.com/trending/money/1k-in-bitcoin-sp-500-gold-which-investment-10-years-ago-won)) with roughly four times the volatility by one industry comparison ([Spark](https://www.spark.money/tools/bitcoin-vs-sp500-returns)), and a buyer at the November 2021 peak above $69,000 ([Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/121763/bitcoin-after-2015-lost-money-bis-report)) is up only about 7% five years later. The best measurement of actual users, a Bank for International Settlements study of crypto apps from 2015 to 2022, found that "a majority of crypto app users in nearly all economies made losses on their bitcoin holdings," with the median user down $431 on $900 invested and large holders selling to small ones before crashes ([BIS](https://www.bis.org/publ/bisbull69.htm); [CoinDesk via Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/retail-crypto-investors-emerging-economies-162932915.html)). In fairness, that snapshot was taken near a cycle low; bitcoin now trades at about 4.4 times its price on FTX's bankruptcy date ([Protos](https://protos.com/ftx-customers-to-be-repaid-at-16871-bitcoin-prices)), so many patient holders from that era are ahead today, though no study has measured realized retail results at 2026 prices.

Beyond bitcoin, the odds deteriorate fast. More than half of the tokens CoinGecko has tracked since 2021 have stopped trading ([CoinGecko](https://www.coingecko.com/research/publications/how-many-cryptocurrencies-failed)), and 98.6% of more than 7 million pump.fun tokens showed rug-pull or pump-and-dump traits in a Solidus Labs review that the platform disputes ([Solidus Labs](https://www.soliduslabs.com/reports/solana-rug-pulls-pump-dumps-crypto-compliance)). Fraud is a separate path to total loss: Americans reported **$11.4 billion in crypto-linked losses in 2025**, averaging $62,604 per complaint, and crypto investment fraud, typically begun by text, social media or dating apps that show victims fake profits, was the single largest source of losses in the FBI's report ([FBI IC3](https://www.ic3.gov/AnnualReport/Reports/2025_IC3Report.pdf)). How you hold crypto decides whether you can get money out. Most spot bitcoin ETFs charge 0.15–0.25% a year (the legacy GBTC charges 1.50%) and sell like any stock in an ordinary or retirement account ([24/7 Wall St.](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/05/22/which-bitcoin-btc-etf-is-best-for-investors/)), while coins left on platforms have been frozen, as at Celsius in 2022 ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/crypto/2022/07/13/celsius-files-bankruptcy-chapter-11-withdrawals/)), or repaid at bankruptcy-date prices, as at FTX ([Protos](https://protos.com/ftx-customers-to-be-repaid-at-16871-bitcoin-prices)). Mainstream guidance keeps the position small: Morgan Stanley caps crypto at 4% for its most aggressive portfolios and 0% for conservative ones, and BlackRock calls a 1–2% bitcoin weight reasonable ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/373562/morgan-stanley-sets-4-crypto-cap-opportunistic-portfolios-aligning-blackrock-grayscale)).

### Leveraged ETFs decay when held longer than a day

Daily-reset leveraged funds deliver their multiple for one day only. In a Wall Street Journal example, a stock that falls 30% and then rises 50% ends up 5%, while a 2x fund on it falls 60% and then doubles to finish down 20% ([WSJ via Tiger Brokers](https://www.itiger.com/news/2577374910)). By a standard approximation, a 2x fund trails twice its index's return by about the square of the index's annual volatility each year: roughly 4 points for an index with 20% volatility and about 36 points for a stock with 60% volatility, before fees near 1% and swap financing. The results match the math. A 2x MicroStrategy fund lost 65% in a year when the stock rose 28% (same source); Morningstar's review of 518 single-stock ETFs through August 2026 found a **median loss of 38% since launch**, with 19% down more than 75% and only 18% beating the stock they track ([Morningstar](https://www.morningstar.com/funds/these-innovative-etfs-have-been-disastrous-investors)); and South Korean retail investors lost about 6.3 trillion won in under three months on newly launched single-stock leveraged products in 2026 ([Asia Business Daily](https://www.asiae.co.kr/en/article/2026102113361689828)). Regulators have said as much since 2009, when FINRA called these funds "typically… unsuitable for retail investors who plan to hold them for longer than one trading session" ([FINRA](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/notices/09-31)), and in December 2025 the SEC declined to review funds above 2x ([WealthManagement.com](https://www.wealthmanagement.com/etfs/sec-halts-high-leveraged-etf-plans-in-warning-over-risks)). Demand keeps climbing anyway, with record inflows of $30.8 billion in the first seven months of 2026 ([Paperjam](https://en.paperjam.lu/article/leveraged-single-stock-etfs-bad-in-up-markets-worst-in-down-markets)).

### Stock pickers are hunting for 46 companies

Hendrik Bessembinder's century-long study of about 30,000 US stocks found that **only 41% beat one-month Treasury bills over their lifetimes**, fewer than half posted positive returns at all, and just 46 companies produced half of the $91 trillion in wealth the market created from 1926 to 2025 ([Morningstar Indexes](https://indexes.morningstar.com/insights/perspective/blt1a0584197b381618/why-most-stocks-arent-worth-owning)). J.P. Morgan found that about 40% of Russell 3000 stocks from 1980 to 2014 suffered a permanent decline of 70% or more ([J.P. Morgan](https://www.chase.com/content/dam/privatebanking/en/mobile/documents/eotm/eotm_2014_09_02_agonyescstasy.pdf)), and that such collapses are hard to see coming: at their peaks, most catastrophic decliners were profitable, carried modest debt and enjoyed analyst consensus tilted toward "strong buy" ([J.P. Morgan](https://www.jpmorgan.com/content/dam/jpmorgan/documents/wealth-management/the-agony-and-the-ecstasy-2024.pdf)). Activity compounds the damage. In Barber and Odean's study of 66,465 brokerage households, the most active traders earned 11.4% a year while the market returned 17.9% ([*Journal of Finance*](https://doi.org/10.1111/0022-1082.00226)), a gap that over 20 years turns $10,000 into about $87,000 instead of $269,000. In the 2021 GameStop frenzy, 67% of Wealthsimple users who traded the stock lost money ([Globe and Mail](https://arc-dev.theglobeandmail.com/investing/markets/inside-the-market/article-young-men-losing-money-wealthsimple-study-profiles-gamestop-investors)). Finding the next Nvidia would change your life, but an index fund owns all 46 winners automatically.

### Option spreads tax every 0DTE round trip

Listed options volume set its sixth straight annual record in 2025, up about 24% ([FOW](https://www.fow.com/insights/us-options-volume-grows-a-quarter-in-2025-to-new-record)), and same-day (0DTE) contracts now dominate retail activity: more than 75% of identified retail S&P 500 option trades in one study were 0DTE ([Beckmeyer, Branger & Gayda](https://wp.lancs.ac.uk/fofi2024/files/2024/04/FoFI-2024-146-Leander-Gayda.pdf)). FINRA's warning is blunt: "any strategy that can quickly earn profits can quickly bring losses as well," buyers can lose the entire premium, and writers of uncovered calls face unlimited losses ([FINRA](https://www.finra.org/investors/insights/zeroing-in-options-trading-strategy)). That 0DTE study, which could identify only a small slice of retail activity, found those traders lost about **$241,000 on an average day, rising to $350,000** after daily expirations began in 2022, with roughly 60% of the losses coming from transaction costs, and a *Journal of Finance* paper on 2019–2021 data found retail investors favor cheap weekly options with bid-ask spreads averaging 12.6% and "lose money on average" ([London Business School](https://lbsresearch.london.edu/id/eprint/2827)). The strongest counter-evidence, a Cboe-funded 2025 paper, found no statistically significant loss on Cboe customer trades held to expiration from 2020 to mid-2023, but its sample coincided with a 33–38% market rise, the profits came from bought calls and sold puts, and losses at intraday horizons were significant ([Amaya, Garcia-Ares, Pearson & Vasquez](https://cdn.cboe.com/resources/education/research_publications/Retail_Profitability.pdf)). The cost arithmetic settles the question for a frequent trader: in that paper's data, effective spreads of about 6% cost roughly 6% of the premium on every round trip, and about 11% on trades under $250, a toll a 0DTE trader pays again and again during hours a day job already claims.

### Prediction markets and sportsbooks pay the house and the makers

Prediction markets pay $1 if an event happens, and before fees "participants simply swap money" ([Bürgi, Deng & Whelan](https://www.karlwhelan.com/Papers/Kalshi.pdf)). They have become a mass-market betting substitute, with Kalshi and Polymarket together trading a record **$50.6 billion in July 2026** ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/news/web3/2026-08-03-kalshi-polymarket-volume-july-410382)) and Robinhood, DraftKings and FanDuel running their own versions ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/07/prediction-markets-take-center-stage-in-latest-quarterly-earnings.html)). The best academic data, covering Kalshi from 2021 to April 2025, shows a strong longshot bias: contracts priced under 10 cents lost more than 60% of the money put in, the average contract returned about −20%, and **takers, who accept posted prices, averaged −31.46% per contract** against −9.64% for makers. Only favorites priced above 70 cents earned a small positive return after fees, and the bias appears to be shrinking (same source). Kalshi's taker fee of 0.07 × price × (1 − price) works out to $1.75 per 100 contracts at 50 cents, about 3.5% of the stake, and about 6.3% of the stake at 10 cents (my arithmetic from the paper's fee formula), so fees bite hardest exactly where the pricing bias already hurts. On Polymarket, 0.55% of profitable maker wallets and 0.26% of winning taker wallets each captured about half of their group's gains, according to a surveillance vendor's study ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2026/04/29/a-tiny-group-is-winning-on-polymarket-as-under-1-of-wallets-take-half-the-profits)). Legality is unsettled: the Third Circuit sided with Kalshi in April 2026, while the Ninth and Sixth Circuits sided with the states in August and September ([Justia](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca3/25-1922/25-1922-2026-04-06.html); [Justia](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca6/26-3196/26-3196-2026-09-25.html); [RotoWire](https://www.rotowire.com/prediction-markets/legal-timeline)), and New Jersey's Supreme Court petition is not expected to reach conference before January 2027 ([SiaPredict](https://siapredict.com/blog/kalshi-supreme-court-flaherty-docket-26-299)). Markets can vanish state by state, as Polymarket's did in Nevada.

Sports betting is negative by design. It is legal in 39 states and DC ([AGA](https://www.americangaming.org/sports-event-contracts/)), and in 2025 sportsbooks kept $16.96 billion of the $166.94 billion wagered ([AGA](https://www.americangaming.org/commercial-gaming-revenue-hits-78-7-billion-in-2025-driving-record-18-1-billion-in-gaming-taxes-nationwide/)), a **hold of about 10.2%**, up from 9.2% in 2024 ([Legal Sports Report](https://www.legalsportsreport.com/263300/sportsbooks-make-more-money-from-slower-sports-betting-growth-in-2025/)). At standard −110 odds a bettor risks $110 to win $100 and must win 52.38% of bets just to break even. The rare bettor who finds an edge gets throttled: in Massachusetts, books limited 0.64% of accounts, with limits skewed toward winners ([Gaming Intelligence](https://www.gamingintelligence.com/sectors/betting/231532-massachusetts-first-to-require-sportsbooks-to-justify-account-limits-on-winning-bettors/)). The household damage is measurable. Legalization reduced savings and investment and raised credit card debt and overdrafts among financially constrained households ([NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/w33108)), and in states with online betting, bankruptcy likelihood rose 25–30% ([UCLA Anderson Review](https://anderson-review.ucla.edu/as-states-permitted-online-sports-gambling-citizens-personal-financial-health-suffered)). The national problem-gambling helpline is 1-800-MY-RESET ([NCPG](https://www.ncpgambling.org/news/1-800-my-reset-announcement/)).

### Speculation belongs in a walled-off 5% account

If the urge to take a swing persists, the practitioner consensus is to cap it and isolate it. Morningstar suggests holding "mad money," an idea traced to Benjamin Graham, to 5% or 10% of a portfolio ([Morningstar](https://morningstar.co.uk/uk/news/211303/what-is-mad-money.aspx)), and Jason Zweig's version adds discipline: keep it in a separate account, never add money after losses, and close the account if the money is gone ([WealthTrack](https://wealthtrack.com/timeless-wisdom/)). A rule consistent with all the evidence above is to hold no more than 5% of investable assets in that account, keep crypto inside the 1–4% range institutions use, prefer spot ETFs to coins on platforms, never hold leveraged funds overnight, and skim any winnings back into the index core. Size it so that losing all of it would annoy you rather than change your life. These caps come from practitioners, some of whom sell crypto products, rather than from academic optimization, so treat them as ceilings, not targets.

## Your Luau skill is the highest-return asset you own

### $500 a month of income equals a 7% return on $86,000

At small balances, earning beats investing. An extra $6,000 a year from side work equals the expected annual gain on about $86,000 invested at 7%, and on a $10,000 portfolio even an exceptional 20% year produces $2,000, about four months of a $500-a-month side income. That is the deepest flaw in day trading a small account: even a genuine edge is a percentage of a small number, while an hour of paid work is worth the same whatever your balance. Surveys temper expectations, though. Bankrate found side hustlers' **median income was $200 a month** against an $885 average, a gap that shows how a few high earners skew the picture ([Bankrate](https://www.bankrate.com/press-releases/fewer-americans-have-a-side-hustle-in-2025/)); LendingTree's 2026 survey reported a $1,242 monthly average, with a majority working 5–15 hours a week ([LendingTree](https://www.lendingtree.com/debt-consolidation/side-hustle-income-survey/)); and the Fed found 70% of gig workers spend under five hours a week on gig work ([Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/publications/2025-economic-well-being-of-us-households-in-2024-employment-and-gig-work.htm)). Self-employment income also swings, with 58% of self-employed adults reporting that it varies month to month ([Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/publications/files/2025-report-economic-well-being-us-households-202605.pdf)), which argues for investing side income rather than budgeting around it.

### Freelance scripting pays dollars now, and your game is the résumé

Skilled services convert hours into dollars with no product risk. Advertised US pay for hourly Lua developer roles averaged **$52.84 an hour** in June 2026, with most between $40 and $65 ([ZipRecruiter](https://www.ziprecruiter.com/Jobs/Hourly-Lua-Developer)). Roblox-specific rates, which come from vendor guides with an interest in the numbers, run $10–25 an hour for newer scripters without live games, **$25–60 for developers with live-game credits**, and $50–150 or more for senior systems engineers; per-job prices range from $50–250 for a well-scoped feature to $1,000–10,000 for a full backend covering saving, monetization and anti-exploit work ([BLOXG](https://bloxg.com/blog/how-much-to-pay-a-roblox-developer); [Roblox DevMatch](https://robloxdevmatch.com/blog/how-to-hire-roblox-scripters); [Game-Ace](https://game-ace.com/blog/how-to-hire-the-best-roblox-game-developer/)). Avoid commissions paid in Robux: a DevForum posting offering 1,800 Robux per task ([DevForum](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/hiring-roblox-scripter-%E2%80%93-1800-robux-per-task/4438381)) pays about $6.84 at the DevEx rate, and nothing at all until your balance reaches 30,000 Robux. On the large marketplaces, Upwork charges freelancers a variable 0–15% fee and Fiverr a flat 20% ([goLance](https://golance.com/blogs/upwork-fees-explained-2026); [GigRadar](https://gigradar.io/blog/upwork-vs-fiverr)), so a $40-an-hour contract nets roughly **$22–28 an hour** after fees and about 30% federal tax, before state tax.

The pivot point in those vendor guides, live-game credits, is the most useful finding for you. The incremental game you are already building, once it is live with real players and working saving, progression and monetization systems, is the credential that moves a scripter out of the bottom band, and Roblox's Talent Hub, its official board for hiring and job searches, is the place to show it ([Roblox Help](https://en.help.roblox.com/hc/en-us/articles/4404630280980-Parental-guidance-on-Talent-Hub)). AI is reshaping which gigs pay. Within eight months of ChatGPT's release, postings for automation-prone coding jobs on freelance platforms fell about 20% relative to manual work ([Demirci, Hannane & Zhu, *Management Science*](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/fpi/10.1287/mnsc.2024.05420)), while Upwork reported freelance earnings from AI-related work up 25% year over year, mostly for experienced people ([Axios](https://www.axios.com/2025/06/30/ai-job-vibe-coding-upwork)). The result is a barbell: small "script this" jobs face falling prices, while systems work and new niches, including AI code-evaluation roles for Lua and Roblox developers ([startup.jobs](https://startup.jobs/copy-of-senior-lua-developer-roblox-ai-code-evaluation-lifted-an-upwork-company-8803203)), still command US-level rates.

### Roblox pays a $1,500 median on a shrinking platform

Roblox pays creators through its Developer Exchange (DevEx) at **$0.0038 per earned Robux**, or $38 per 10,000, after an 8.5% increase in September 2025; cashing out requires 30,000 earned Robux, about $114, and an account holder aged 13 or older ([Roblox Creator Hub](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/earning-on-roblox); [Roblox 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1315098/000131509826000024/rblx-20251231.htm)). Creators keep 70% of Robux spent in their games, so a 400-Robux game pass nets 280 Robux, about $1.06. Since June 8, 2026, spending by age-checked US adults on game passes, developer products, subscriptions, private servers and paid access converts at **$0.0054**, 42% more, in games set to R15 avatars or meeting alternatives that include showing "no visible player characters," a bar an incremental game can clear by switching its avatar setting to R15 Only or by showing no characters at all ([DevForum](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/introducing-the-us-18-devex-rate-earn-42-more-on-spend-from-18-us-players/4607091)). US adults make up 37% of US age-checked daily users and spend more than 50% more than minors ([Roblox Q3 2026 letter](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1315098/000162828026068235/ex991-q32026earningsshar.htm)). Across the platform, creators received about 24 cents of every bookings dollar in the third quarter (same source).

The payout distribution is the sobering part. In the year to June 30, 2026, Roblox paid about $1.7 billion through DevEx; the top 10 creators averaged about $65.7 million, the top 1,000 about $1.4 million, and the **median among more than 42,000 participants received about $1,500** ([IBTimes UK](https://www.ibtimes.co.uk/roblox-top-creators-devex-payouts-2026-1819403); [Roblox Newsroom](https://about.roblox.com/newsroom/2026/09/global-impact-of-creation-on-roblox)). By rough arithmetic, the top 1,000 took about 80% of the pool. That median covers only creators who reached the cash-out threshold: about 23,500 were actually paid in 2025, out of what Roblox describes as millions of monetizing creators ([Roblox 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1315098/000131509826000024/rblx-20251231.htm); [Roblox Creator Hub](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/earning-on-roblox)). The platform is also contracting. In the third quarter of 2026, daily users fell 9% to 138 million, bookings fell 12%, monthly unique payers fell 16% and DevEx payouts fell 6%, and Roblox guided fourth-quarter bookings down 10–15% ([Roblox Q3 2026 letter](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1315098/000162828026068235/ex991-q32026earningsshar.htm)). The rules keep moving too: Creator Rewards replaced Premium Payouts in July 2025, Plus replaced Premium in April 2026, and discovery signals changed in June 2026 ([Roblox Creator Hub](https://create.roblox.com/docs/en-us/creator-rewards.md); [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/04/13/roblox-plus-creator-payouts/); [Dubit](https://dubit.io/library/what-makes-a-new-roblox-game-take-off)). Competition is accelerating as well, with Roblox's prompt-to-game tool producing more than 70,000 published games in its alpha test alone ([Roblox Q3 2026 letter](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1315098/000162828026068235/ex991-q32026earningsshar.htm)).

### Retention, not size, decides which small games break out

Breakouts still happen, and the ranking system lets small games compete. About 40% of the weekly top 100 games by recommendation impressions in the third quarter were under three months old ([Roblox Q3 2026 letter](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1315098/000162828026068235/ex991-q32026earningsshar.htm)), and *Grow a Garden*, an idle-style farming game whose first version was reportedly built by a 16-year-old in about three days, passed 20 million concurrent players ([IBTimes UK](https://www.ibtimes.co.uk/roblox-top-creators-devex-payouts-2026-1819403)). Roblox's recommendations use per-user averages, so small games with engaged players are not penalized, and the signals it lists as most important are play-through rate, first-play bounce within 60 and 180 seconds, play days per user across day one, days two to seven and days eight to 28, and playtime per user capped at 60 minutes a day ([Roblox Creator Hub](https://create.roblox.com/docs/en-us/discovery.md)). That profile suits an incremental game's daily-return loop, provided the first minute hooks new players, because idle hours beyond the 60-minute cap earn no ranking credit. New games now reach only age-checked users 16 and older until they log 250 unique plays by highly engaged age-checked players within 60 days, the evaluation that opens the younger Kids and Select audiences ([Roblox Creator Hub](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/publishing/kids-and-select)). Revenue layers beyond passes and developer products include Creator Rewards of 5 Robux a day for each recent spender who plays at least 10 minutes and picks your game among the first three they open that day ([Roblox Creator Hub](https://create.roblox.com/docs/en-us/creator-rewards.md)), and rewarded video ads once a game passes 2,000 monthly unique visitors ([DevForum](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/rewarded-video-ads-are-now-available-to-all-ads-eligible-creators/4063278)). The one Roblox route that pays dollars directly is the Creator Store, where plugins priced at $4.99–$249.99 and models at $2.99–$49.99 earn **100% of net proceeds** after a 30-day escrow ([Roblox Creator Hub](https://create.roblox.com/docs/en-us/production/creator-store.md)), so systems you have already built can be packaged and sold.

### Other products and gigs have low medians and fat tails

Building outside Roblox follows the same pattern. The median Steam game released in 2025 grossed **$249–$318** by year-end, about 40% failed to recoup the $100 listing fee, and about 10% grossed $50,000 or more ([80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/analysts-report-drop-in-game-revenue-on-steam-despite-growing-number-of-releases); [Game Dev Report](https://gamedevreport.beehiiv.com/p/19-606-steam-games-made-16b-in-2025-most-made-0-but-that-s-not-a-problem)). About 90% of roughly 70,000 Fortnite creators earned under $1,000 in 2024, and 0.08% earned $1 million or more ([Naavik](https://naavik.co/deep-dives/the-state-of-ugc-games-2026/)). Only 17% of new subscription apps reached $1,000 a month in recurring revenue in 2025 ([RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2026/)). Every one of these datasets counts only finished, launched products, so the odds per attempt are worse still. YouTube requires 1,000 subscribers and 4,000 watch hours before paying any ad revenue, and new applicants will need 8,000 hours from February 1, 2027 ([Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/08/10/youtube-partner-program-ad-eligibility-requirements-shorts/)). At the low-skill end, pay is modest but immediate: tutors earned a median $19.27 an hour in 2024 ([BLS](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/education-training-and-library/tutors.htm)), and rideshare nets roughly $11–23 an hour after vehicle costs, depending on who funds the study ([EPI](https://www.epi.org/?p=145552); [HR&A Advisors](https://www.hraadvisors.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/HRA_Uber-Rideshare-Driver-Net-Earnings-Study_2025_Philadelphia.pdf)). A developer's freelance rate beats both.

### Side profit loses about 30% to federal tax

Side profit is self-employment income, which owes 15.3% self-employment tax (12.4% for Social Security up to a $184,500 wage base shared with your paycheck, plus 2.9% for Medicare) on top of income tax ([Journal of Accountancy](https://www.journalofaccountancy.com/news/2025/oct/social-security-wage-base-and-cola-announced-for-2026/)). For a single filer in the 22% bracket who qualifies for the 20% qualified business income deduction, which the 2025 tax law made permanent ([IRS](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/understanding-the-working-families-tax-cuts-business-tax-provisions-youtube-video-text-script)), $500 of profit owes about $152 in federal tax, or **30.5%**; in the 12% bracket the figure is about 23% (my arithmetic). Fewer forms will arrive. For 2026, clients file a 1099-NEC only for payments of $2,000 or more ([IRS Publication 1099](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p1099)), and payment apps file a 1099-K only above $20,000 and 200 transactions, but the IRS is explicit that you "must still report any income on your tax return" ([IRS](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/understanding-your-form-1099-k)). If you expect to owe $1,000 or more, you generally need estimated payments, though you avoid penalties by covering 90% of this year's tax or 100% of last year's (110% above $150,000 of income) through withholding and estimates ([IRS](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/estimated-taxes)), which a W-2 employee can often handle simply by raising paycheck withholding. Self-employment income also opens a Solo 401(k) or SEP with total additions up to $72,000 for 2026, but elective deferrals are limited "by person, not by plan," so they share the $24,500 cap with your day-job 401(k) ([IRS](https://www.irs.gov/retirement-plans/one-participant-401k-plans); [SelfEmployed.com](https://www.selfemployed.com/news/solo-401k-sep-ira-limits-72000-2026/)). Set aside about 30% of side profit, plus state tax, the day it arrives.

## Under 18, custodians and age gates set the rules

For a minor, earned income is the key that opens the best account. A custodial Roth IRA has no minimum age but requires the child's own earned income, caps contributions at the lesser of that income or $7,500 for 2026, and, like an adult Roth, lets contributions come back out at any time ([NerdWallet](https://www.nerdwallet.com/retirement/learn/why-your-kid-needs-a-roth-ira); [Vanguard](https://investor.vanguard.com/accounts-plans/iras/custodial-ira); [Fidelity](https://www.fidelity.com/learning-center/personal-finance/trump-accounts)). Wages from a job qualify; whether DevEx or freelance profit counts as the child's earned income is a question for a tax preparer, because this research did not establish how Roblox payouts are classified. A custodial UTMA or UGMA brokerage account has no contribution cap, but the money legally becomes the child's, and 2026 investment income above $2,700 is taxed at the parent's rate under the kiddie tax ([Fidelity](https://www.fidelity.com/learning-center/personal-finance/trump-accounts); [SmartAsset](https://smartasset.com/taxes/kiddie-tax)). The new Trump accounts, open since July 4, 2026, accept up to $5,000 a year per child, must hold low-cost US stock index funds, and lock the money until 18, so they are worth opening mainly to capture free money such as the $1,000 federal seed for citizens born from 2025 through 2028, not as accessible side savings ([Fidelity](https://www.fidelity.com/learning-center/personal-finance/trump-accounts); [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/01/trump-accounts-launch-july-4.html)).

Roblox has its own age ladder. Under 13, a developer can build, publish and earn Robux but cannot cash out; DevEx opens at 13 ([Roblox Creator Hub](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/earning-on-roblox)). Selling plugins or models on the Creator Store requires being 18, or 13 to 17 with a parent or guardian completing the Stripe payout setup ([Roblox Creator Hub](https://create.roblox.com/docs/en-us/production/creator-store.md)); uploading avatar items requires government ID or a linked, ID-verified parent account ([Roblox Creator Hub](https://create.roblox.com/docs/marketplace/marketplace-fees-and-commissions)); and creators under 18 must pass facial age estimation, among other steps, to publish to the Kids and Select audiences ([Roblox Creator Hub](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/publishing/kids-and-select)). Talent Hub, Roblox's official board for finding collaborators and paid work, publishes separate guidance for parents ([Roblox Help](https://en.help.roblox.com/hc/en-us/articles/4404630280980-Parental-guidance-on-Talent-Hub)). Because a minor's investments sit in accounts an adult controls, the speculative products above are largely out of reach for a teenager, which, given the evidence, costs very little.

## Conclusion

The evidence reframes the original choice. The meaningful line runs not between safe and risky but between positive-sum and zero-sum: an index fund pays you a share of corporate growth and freelance work pays you for value delivered, while options, event contracts, sportsbooks and day trades shuffle money between participants, minus a toll, toward whoever has the better information, infrastructure or pricing power, which is rarely someone checking a phone between meetings. Willingness to take risk is therefore worth something only where risk is paid: holding stocks through 34–57% crashes, which is what earns the equity premium, and betting on your own skills, where the downside is your evenings and the upside is open-ended. Combining the two is the realistic route to "a lot." Ten paid hours a week at a $25 net rate is roughly $1,000 a month, and that sum invested at 7% becomes about $500,000 in 20 years, an outcome no trading strategy in the evidence delivers to a typical person.
